Glossary

Dry-type vs. liquid-filled

Two ways of insulating and cooling a transformer.

Definition

Two ways of insulating and cooling a transformer. Liquid-filled units carry more load in less volume and generally last longer; dry-type units have no fluid to contain and so avoid the fire and spill provisions that push liquid units outdoors or into vaults.

In practice

A liquid-filled transformer sits in a tank of mineral oil or a less flammable fluid. The liquid insulates the windings and carries their heat to the tank walls and radiators. A dry-type transformer has no liquid: its windings are insulated with varnish or cast in resin and cooled by air moving over them.

Liquid is the better coolant and the better insulator, so liquid-filled units are smaller, more efficient and longer-lived for a given rating, and they dominate above a few MVA and outdoors. The liquid is also the drawback indoors, where it brings requirements for containment and fire separation. Dry-type units avoid those and are the usual choice inside buildings, at the cost of size, losses and a lower tolerance for overload.

Worth knowing

  • Less-flammable fluids such as natural esters allow liquid-filled units in some indoor locations that mineral oil does not.
  • Dry-type units need clean, dry air; dust and moisture on the windings shorten their life.
