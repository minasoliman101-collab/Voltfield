MCCB (Molded-Case Circuit Breaker)

An overcurrent protection device typically rated up to about 2,500 A, sitting between miniature breakers and large power breakers.

Definition

An overcurrent protection device typically rated up to about 2,500 A, sitting between miniature breakers and large power breakers.

In practice

A molded-case breaker has its contacts, mechanism and trip unit sealed inside an insulating case. It is the breaker found in panelboards and switchboards and feeding most equipment in a commercial or industrial building. It sits between the miniature breakers used on small branch circuits and the large drawout power breakers used in switchgear.

The trip unit decides how it behaves. A thermal-magnetic unit has a bimetal element for overloads and a magnetic element for short circuits, with little adjustment. An electronic unit measures the current and applies adjustable long-time, short-time, instantaneous and ground-fault settings, which is what makes it possible to coordinate one breaker with those above and below it.

Worth knowing

  • An MCCB is sealed and not meant to be opened or maintained internally; a power breaker is.
  • Unless marked as 100 percent rated, it may carry only 80 percent of its rating continuously.
