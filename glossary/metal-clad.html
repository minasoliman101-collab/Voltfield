Metal-clad vs. metal-enclosed

Both describe switchgear in a grounded metal enclosure, but metal-clad adds specific requirements: drawout breakers, insulated bus, and grounded metal barriers between compartments.

Definition

Both describe switchgear in a grounded metal enclosure, but metal-clad adds specific requirements: drawout breakers, insulated bus, and grounded metal barriers between compartments. Those barriers decide what can be worked on safely while the lineup stays energized. See the switchgear compartments guide.

In practice

Metal-enclosed is the broad term: any switchgear whose live parts are inside a grounded metal housing. Metal-clad is a specific, more demanding construction defined in the standards, used mainly at medium voltage. Every metal-clad lineup is metal-enclosed; most metal-enclosed lineups are not metal-clad.

To be metal-clad, the breakers must be removable, with a mechanism that moves them between connected, test and disconnected positions; the main parts of each circuit must sit in separate compartments divided by grounded metal barriers; shutters must cover the live contacts automatically when a breaker is withdrawn; and the bus must be insulated. Each requirement is there to limit how far a fault can spread and what a person can touch.

Worth knowing

  • The governing standards are IEEE C37.20.2 for metal-clad and C37.20.3 for metal-enclosed interrupter switchgear.
  • Arc-resistant construction is a further, separate rating and should not be assumed from either term.
