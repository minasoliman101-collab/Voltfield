Glossary

Ampacity

The current a conductor can carry continuously without exceeding its temperature rating.

Definition

The current a conductor can carry continuously without exceeding its temperature rating. The table value is a starting point, not an answer: it derates for ambient temperature and for how many conductors are bundled together, which is what turns a calculated size into an installed one.

In practice

Ampacity is not a property of the wire alone. The same conductor has a different ampacity in free air, in a conduit with two others, in a conduit with eight others, and in a hot attic. The published figure assumes a stated ambient temperature and no more than three current-carrying conductors together, and each departure from those conditions lowers it.

There is also a second limit that has nothing to do with the conductor. Equipment terminals are rated for 60 or 75°C, and the circuit is limited by the lowest-rated termination in it. A conductor insulated for 90°C can use its higher rating for derating arithmetic, but it still has to be sized so the terminal stays within its own limit.

Worth knowing

  • Ampacity is a thermal limit: it is the current at which the insulation reaches its rated temperature.
  • Two checks decide a conductor size, the termination rating and the derated ampacity, and the larger answer governs.
  • On long runs voltage drop, not ampacity, usually sets the size.
