Grid-forming vs. grid-following

Grid-forming inverters/PCS can establish their own voltage and frequency reference and operate independent of the grid.

Definition

Grid-forming inverters/PCS can establish their own voltage and frequency reference and operate independent of the grid. Grid-following equipment instead synchronizes to an existing grid signal. Both appear on the Battery Storage sector's Power Conversion category. See Grid-Forming vs. Grid-Following Inverters.

In practice

A grid-following inverter measures the voltage it is connected to, locks onto it, and injects current in step with it. It behaves as a controlled current source and needs a grid to exist first. A grid-forming inverter behaves as a voltage source: it sets its own voltage and frequency and lets the current be whatever the system demands, as a synchronous generator does.

For years almost all inverters were grid-following, which worked while conventional generators set the voltage. As inverter-based generation grows and synchronous machines retire, parts of the grid become weak, meaning the voltage moves easily when current changes, and grid-following controls can become unstable there. Grid-forming inverters hold the voltage steady in those conditions and can restart a dead system.

Worth knowing

  • Grid-forming is a control method, and largely a matter of firmware; the power hardware is similar.
  • A grid-forming inverter can only help with a disturbance if it has energy and current headroom in reserve at the time.
