SoC and SoH

State of charge is how full a battery is right now; state of health is how much of its original capacity remains after aging.

Definition

State of charge is how full a battery is right now; state of health is how much of its original capacity remains after aging. The battery management system estimates both, and the accuracy of those estimates determines usable capacity and whether warranty terms are met.

In practice

State of charge is the fuel gauge: the energy in the battery now, as a percentage of what it can hold now. State of health is the size of the tank: what it can hold now, as a percentage of what it held when new. A battery at 100 percent state of charge and 80 percent state of health is full, and holds four fifths of its original energy.

Neither can be measured directly. The battery management system infers both from voltage, current and temperature, mainly by adding up the current that has flowed in and out and correcting the total when the battery rests. Errors accumulate, and they matter: operators keep margins at the top and bottom of the range to allow for them, which reduces the capacity that can be used.

Worth knowing

  • End of life is commonly defined as a state of health of 70 or 80 percent, depending on the contract.
  • State of charge also has to be balanced between cells, or the weakest cell limits the whole string.
