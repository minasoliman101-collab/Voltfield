CRAH vs. CRAC

Both are precision room-level cooling units.

Definition

Both are precision room-level cooling units. A CRAH uses chilled water supplied from a plant; a CRAC has its own refrigeration circuit. Sizing turns on sensible heat ratio rather than nominal tonnage, because a data hall load carries almost no latent component.

In practice

The whole difference is where the cold is made. A computer room air conditioner has a compressor and a refrigerant circuit inside the cabinet and rejects its heat to an outdoor condenser or a pumped loop. A computer room air handler has only a coil, fans and a control valve, and takes chilled water from a central plant.

That one difference drives the rest. CRAC units are independent, so each fails alone, and they suit rooms with no chiller plant. CRAH units depend on the plant behind them, so their redundancy has to be built into the chillers, pumps and pipework, but at scale the plant is more efficient and can use free cooling for much of the year.

Worth knowing

  • Both are rated on sensible cooling at a stated return air temperature, and deliver less if cold air is allowed to mix into the return.
  • CRAH fans draw little enough to be kept on a UPS; compressors generally are not.
