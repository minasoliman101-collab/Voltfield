Glossary

C-rate

The rate a battery charges or discharges relative to its capacity.

Definition

The rate a battery charges or discharges relative to its capacity. A 1C rate empties the system in one hour, 0.5C in two, 0.25C in four. It is the link between a project's power rating in MW and its energy rating in MWh, and it drives cell selection and thermal design. See BESS C-Rate Explained for a worked example.

In practice

C-rate normalizes current to the size of the battery, so that cells of different sizes can be compared. A 100 Ah cell discharged at 100 A is at 1C; the same cell at 25 A is at 0.25C. For a whole system it is easier to think in power and energy: a 100 MW, 400 MWh project is a 0.25C system, more often described as four-hour storage.

The rate matters because batteries are less efficient and age faster when worked hard. A high C-rate means more heat in the cells, more capacity that cannot be reached before the voltage limit, and more cooling. Cells are designed toward one end or the other: energy cells for long, gentle discharges and power cells for short, intense ones, and using either outside its range shortens its life.

Worth knowing

  • Duration in hours is one divided by the C-rate.
  • Charge and discharge rates are specified separately and are often not equal.
