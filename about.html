About Voltfield

A sourcing reference, not a storefront

Voltfield helps engineers and buyers across data center power, renewables, battery storage, oil & gas, and industrial MRO find the right spec, understand real lead times, and connect with the distributor or mill that can actually fulfill an order — without pretending to be the one shipping it.

What Voltfield is

Voltfield is an independent sourcing-reference and engineering-tools platform. It isn't a distributor and doesn't warehouse or ship parts itself — the catalog exists so you can configure an exact spec, see realistic pricing and lead-time reference data, and get pointed toward the real distributor, mill, or OEM that would fulfill it. Exactly how that reference data is compiled is covered in full on the methodology page.

What's actually on the site

Sourcing reference

A configurable catalog across five sectors, the Lead-Time Index, a supplier directory, and catalog-wide analytics — all indicative reference data, not live inventory feeds.

Engineering tools

20+ engineering calculators, a one-line design practice sandbox, and PCB build/layout tools — free, no signup, aimed at early-stage sizing and hands-on practice rather than stamped final designs.

Procurement tools

A BOM generator, BOM Match for existing parts lists, and an RFQ readiness toolkit with copy-paste spec templates for the vendors you actually contact.

Reference & compliance

A glossary of the standards and acronyms that show up across these sectors, plus guides on interconnection, domestic-content rules, FEOC compliance, and equipment safety codes.

How Voltfield is positioned

This wasn't always the framing. The site originally read like a parts storefront — "sold and fulfilled directly," add-to-cart language, checkout flows. That's been rewritten sitewide: what's left is a sourcing-inquiry model instead of a purchase model. You can still configure a part, save it to a sourcing list, and send a request — it just gets routed to a person who connects you with the actual fulfilling distributor, mill, or OEM, rather than treated as a completed sale.
