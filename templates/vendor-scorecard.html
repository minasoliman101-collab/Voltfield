Vendor Scorecard Template for Equipment Suppliers

Seven measures, each tied to a date or a document, weighted and totaled out of five. Fill it in here, then print it or save it as a PDF.

A vendor scorecard records how a supplier performed on real orders, in a form that can be compared between suppliers and over time. It is only as good as its evidence. Each measure here is something that can be read off the order file: the delivery date against the acknowledged date, the number of drawing resubmittals, whether the factory test passed first time. The reasoning is in how to qualify an equipment vendor.

Printing an empty form gives you a blank template. What you type stays in this browser and is not sent anywhere.

What goes in each field

Measures and weights
Seven measures are plenty. Set the weights to reflect what hurts your projects most, and keep them the same for every vendor of the same kind of equipment so the totals can be compared. They should add to 100.
Evidence
Write the fact the score rests on: the two dates, the count, the document. A score with nothing in this column is an opinion. The expediting log and the proof of delivery hold most of what you need.
Score
Use the scale printed under the table and define it before you score anyone. Deciding in advance what a 3 means for on-time delivery, for example up to two weeks late, removes most of the argument.
Weighted and total
Each line is weight times score, and the column adds to a figure out of 5. The total is a summary, not a verdict: a vendor with a 4.2 that failed on one critical measure may still be the wrong choice.
Problems and corrective actions
The most useful box on the page. A low score with an agreed fix is worth more than a high score nobody discussed.
Shared with vendor
Send it and talk it through. Vendors improve what they know is measured, and they will correct dates you have wrong.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Scoring from memory at the end of the year.
  • Blaming the vendor for delay the buyer caused with late drawing comments or late payments.
  • Different weights for different vendors, which makes the totals meaningless side by side.
  • Keeping the scorecard private.
  • Never acting on a poor score.

Questions

What is a vendor scorecard?

A record of a supplier’s performance against a fixed set of measures, such as delivery, quality, documentation and responsiveness, scored and usually weighted so suppliers can be compared and tracked over time.

What KPIs should a supplier scorecard include?

For engineered equipment: on-time delivery against the acknowledged date, drawing turnaround, factory test results, condition on arrival and at site testing, completeness of documentation, responsiveness, and the number and merit of change orders.

How is a weighted vendor score calculated?

Multiply each measure’s score by its weight as a fraction, and add the results. With weights that total 100 percent and scores from 1 to 5, the total is a figure out of 5.

How often should vendors be scored?

At the close of each significant order, with a roll-up review of the approved vendor list once a year.
