Purchase Order Template for Electrical and Power Equipment

A purchase order built for engineered equipment, with the terms a generic PO form has no room for. Fill it in here, then print it or save it as a PDF.

A purchase order is the buyer’s written offer to buy: what, how many, at what price, delivered where and when, and on what terms. Once the vendor accepts it, it is the contract both sides work to. For a pallet of fittings a one-page form is enough. For a transformer or a lineup of switchgear the price is the easy part, and the arguments later are about drawings, tests, documents and dates. This template gives those their own section.

What goes in each field

PO number, date and revision
One number per order, never reused. When anything changes after acceptance, issue the same number with the next revision and state what changed. Two documents with the same number and different contents is how invoices get paid against the wrong price.
Buyer, vendor, ship to
Use legal entity names, the ones on the contract and the tax forms. The ship-to is very often not the buyer’s address: give the site, the gate or laydown area, and a person with a phone number.
Vendor quote number
Tie the order to the exact quote and its revision. If the order departs from the quote anywhere, say so on the line concerned, because a bare reference pulls in everything the quote said, including the vendor’s exceptions.
Incoterms rule and named place
The three letters are only half of it. The rule says who arranges and pays for carriage and, more to the point, where risk passes. It means nothing without the place: “DAP Taylor, TX site, on truck” tells you the vendor carries the risk to your gate and you unload. Incoterms say nothing about title or payment, so state those separately.
Required on site
Give the date you need the equipment, not the date you would like the vendor to ship. If the vendor’s acknowledged date differs, the difference needs resolving before fabrication starts, not when the crane is booked.
Line items
Describe each line so that someone who never saw the quote could check the delivery against it: rating, voltage, key options and the specification or data sheet that carries the rest. Services such as witness tests, commissioning support and training get lines of their own so they can be invoiced and closed separately.
Approval drawings
State when drawings are due, how long you take to return them and that nothing is built before release. On long-lead equipment the drawing cycle is where schedule is most often lost, and it is lost on the buyer’s side as often as the vendor’s.
Factory tests and witness points
Name the tests and the standard they are run to, and say which ones you intend to witness and how much notice you need. A witness point that is not in the order is a favor the vendor may or may not grant.
Documents to be delivered
List them and tie the last payment to them. Test reports and as-built drawings are far easier to get before the final invoice is paid than after.
Late delivery
If late delivery will cost you money, put the remedy in the order. Liquidated damages need a rate, a cap and a clear trigger date. Have counsel check the wording, because an unenforceable clause is worse than none.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Ordering “per quote” and so accepting every exception the vendor took to your specification.
  • An Incoterms rule with no named place, or FOB used for truck freight when the rule is written for sea freight. For containers and trucks, FCA, CPT, DAP or DDP are the rules that fit.
  • A required date with no consequence for missing it and no milestone dates before it.
  • Paying 100% on delivery, which leaves nothing to hold against missing test reports and manuals.
  • Changing scope by email and never revising the order.

Questions

Is a purchase order a contract?

It becomes one when the vendor accepts it, whether by signing it, sending an order acknowledgment or starting the work. Until then it is an offer. That is why the acceptance block and the vendor’s acknowledgment matter: check the acknowledgment against the order line by line, because differing terms on it can change the deal.

What is the difference between a purchase order and an invoice?

The purchase order comes from the buyer before the work and says what is being bought. The invoice comes from the vendor afterwards and asks for payment. Accounts payable matches the invoice to the order and to proof that the goods arrived, which is the three-way match.

What should an equipment purchase order include that an ordinary one does not?

The governing specification and data sheets, the approval drawing cycle, factory tests and witness points, the documents to be handed over, payment milestones tied to those events, the warranty start, and what happens if delivery is late.

Can I use this template as it stands?

It is a general template for laying out an order clearly. It is not legal advice and it does not replace your company’s terms and conditions. Have the wording that matters to you, such as warranty, liability and damages, reviewed by someone qualified to do so.
