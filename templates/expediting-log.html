Expediting Log Template for Equipment Orders

One page per order that shows where it really stands: planned, forecast and actual dates for each milestone, and a record of every promise. Fill it in here, then print it or save it as a PDF.

Expediting is the work of making sure an order arrives when it was promised, by checking progress against milestones while there is still time to act. A delivery date slips long before the delivery: when drawings come back late, when core steel or bushings are not ordered, when the test bay is double-booked. This log tracks those earlier dates, so a slip shows up months ahead of the truck.

Printing an empty form gives you a blank template. What you type stays in this browser and is not sent anywhere.

Planned dates are those agreed at order. Forecast dates are the current best estimate. A forecast later than plan needs a note saying why and what is being done about it.

What goes in each field

Required on site and acknowledged date
Two different dates. The first is what the project needs. The second is what the vendor committed to in its order acknowledgment. The gap between them is the float, and the log exists to protect it.
Planned, forecast, actual
Planned dates are fixed at the start and never edited. Forecast dates are updated at every contact. Actual dates are filled in when a milestone is evidenced, not when it is reported. Keeping all three shows how the order drifted and when, which matters if delay damages are ever discussed.
Approval drawings
The first place schedule is lost, and the buyer’s turnaround is half of it. Log both the submittal and the release. A vendor is entitled to more time if the buyer sat on the drawings.
Long-lead materials ordered
The most useful early check. Ask for copies of the vendor’s purchase orders for critical materials with the prices removed. A vendor that has not ordered core steel or bushings cannot meet the date, whatever its schedule says.
Factory acceptance test
Confirm the test date early enough to book travel, and confirm the test bay is actually reserved. See FAT vs. SAT.
Status
Keep it to a few words used the same way every time: Done, On schedule, At risk, Late. “At risk” means the forecast still meets the need date but something specific threatens it.
Contact record
Date, name and what was promised. People keep promises that they know were written down. It is also the record you will want if the order ends in a claim.
Final documents
An order is not finished when the truck arrives. Test reports, as-built drawings and manuals are deliverables, and the last payment should wait for them.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Asking “is everything on schedule?” and logging the answer. Ask for evidence of a specific milestone.
  • Overwriting the planned date with the new forecast, which erases the slip.
  • Starting to expedite a month before delivery, when nothing can be recovered.
  • Tracking the vendor and ignoring the buyer’s own late drawing comments and late payments.
  • No record of who said what.

Questions

What is expediting in procurement?

Following up on a purchase order after it is placed to make sure the goods are delivered on time, by tracking the supplier’s progress against agreed milestones and acting early when one slips.

What is the difference between desk expediting and field expediting?

Desk expediting is done by phone and email, working from the supplier’s reports and documents. Field or shop expediting means visiting the factory to see progress in person. Critical, long-lead equipment usually justifies visits at key milestones.

How often should an order be expedited?

In proportion to its criticality and how close it is to a milestone. A long-lead order on the critical path is typically checked every two to four weeks early on and weekly as testing and shipment approach.

What should an expediting report contain?

The order and equipment, the required and acknowledged dates, each milestone with planned, forecast and actual dates, the current status, the specific risks to the date and the actions agreed with names and dates.
