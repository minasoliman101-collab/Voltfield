Bid Tabulation Template for Equipment Bids

Three bids on one page and on one basis, with totals that add themselves and a place for the reasons. Fill it in here, then print it or save it as a PDF.

A bid tabulation sets the bids side by side, line by line, so that differences in scope, cost and terms can be seen and the reason for the award is on record. The first table levels the price: every bidder gets a figure on every line, including the lines a bidder left out. The second table holds what cannot be reduced to a price. The method behind it is in how to compare equipment bids.

Printing an empty form gives you a blank template. What you type stays in this browser and is not sent anywhere.

What goes in each field

Bidder names
Three columns suit the usual three bids. For more, run a second sheet with the same lines.
Price lines
Every bidder gets a number on every line. If a bidder included an item in its base price, enter zero and say so under leveling allowances. If a bidder left an item out, get its price in writing; failing that, enter an allowance and mark it as one.
Loss evaluation
For transformers, multiply each bidder’s guaranteed no-load loss by your A factor and its load loss by your B factor, for all units, and enter the results as price lines. They are then part of the total. For other equipment use these lines for whatever operating cost you evaluate, or leave them empty.
Total evaluated cost
The column totals add as you type. This is the figure to compare, and it is not what you will pay: the loss lines are a valuation, not an invoice.
Technically compliant
Decided by the engineer, before the prices are discussed if you can manage it. A bid that is not compliant and cannot be made so is set aside, whatever its price.
Lead time
Write what the lead time runs from. Thirty weeks from order and thirty weeks from drawing approval can be two months apart.
Exceptions
Copy them from the bid in the bidder’s own words, and record how each was resolved: accepted, priced or withdrawn.
Basis of recommendation
One or two sentences that would make sense to an auditor a year from now. If the recommended bid is not the lowest evaluated cost, say what outweighed it.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Comparing cover-page totals before leveling the scope.
  • Leaving a line blank for a bidder, which the total then treats as zero.
  • Recording lead times without what they run from.
  • Leaving losses out of a transformer comparison.
  • Showing one bidder another bidder’s numbers.

Questions

What is a bid tabulation?

A table that lists each bidder’s price, scope, technical data, delivery and terms side by side on the same basis. It is used to compare bids fairly and to record why the award was made.

What is bid leveling?

Adjusting bids so they cover the same scope before comparing them: adding prices for items a bidder left out, separating options a bidder bundled in, and putting all prices on the same delivery basis.

What is total evaluated cost?

The bid price plus the buyer’s valuation of costs that differ between bids but are not in the price, such as the cost of transformer losses over the service life. Bids are ranked on this figure, though the contract is placed at the bid price.

Who should prepare the bid tabulation?

Usually procurement prepares the commercial part and the responsible engineer the technical part, and both sign. Splitting the two helps keep the technical judgment from being pulled by the price.
