Proof of Delivery Template for Equipment Deliveries

A delivery receipt with a receiving inspection for heavy equipment. Fill it in at the truck, then print it or save it as a PDF.

A proof of delivery is the signed record that a shipment arrived: what was delivered, when, to whom, and in what condition. It is the last document in the chain that starts with the purchase order, and it is the one that decides a freight claim. Whatever damage or shortage is not written on it before the driver leaves is very hard to recover afterwards. This version adds the checks that matter for electrical equipment.

Printing an empty form gives you a blank template. What you type stays in this browser and is not sent anywhere.

Signature by the receiver confirms the count and the apparent condition recorded above, and nothing more. It is not acceptance of the equipment under the purchase order.

What goes in each field

Arrival and departure times
Record both. They settle detention charges, and the arrival time shows whether a delivery window was met.
BOL, PRO and PO numbers
These three tie the receipt to the shipment, the carrier’s record and the order. Accounts payable needs the PO number to match the receipt to the invoice.
Quantity on BOL against quantity received
Count handling units yourself before signing. If the count is short, write the shortage on the driver’s copy as well as your own.
Condition and exceptions
Write what you see in plain words: “crate corner crushed”, “two radiator fins bent, north side”. A vague note such as “subject to inspection” on its own carries little weight. Specific notes on the delivery receipt are what a freight claim is built on.
Impact recorders
Large transformers and other heavy equipment are often shipped with an impact recorder or shock indicators. Read them with the driver present and before the load comes off the trailer, because once it is on the ground nobody can say when a shock happened. If a reading is over the manufacturer’s limit, note it, tell the manufacturer and follow their instructions before unloading or moving the unit.
Pressure and level readings
Equipment shipped sealed or gas-filled should arrive with its pressure still positive. A lost charge suggests a leak and possible moisture ingress. Record the reading and the ambient temperature.
Photos
Take them on the truck. Photos of damage taken after unloading invite the answer that the damage happened during unloading.
Signatures
Get the driver’s printed name and signature next to any exception. The receiver’s signature confirms count and visible condition only; acceptance under the purchase order comes later, after inspection and testing.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Signing clean and reporting damage afterwards. A clean receipt is evidence that the freight arrived in good order.
  • Unloading before reading impact recorders.
  • Refusing a damaged shipment outright when it could be received with exceptions noted, which usually leaves the receiver in a stronger position and the equipment in safer hands. Check your contract and call the vendor first.
  • Noting damage on your copy but not on the copy the driver takes away.
  • Treating the delivery signature as acceptance of the equipment.

Questions

What is a proof of delivery?

A document signed by the receiver, and usually the driver, confirming that a shipment was delivered: what arrived, the quantity, the date and time, and the condition it was in. It is also called a delivery receipt.

What is the difference between a POD and a BOL?

The bill of lading is issued at pickup and records what the carrier took on. The proof of delivery is signed at the destination and records what arrived. The consignee’s signed copy of the bill of lading often serves as the proof of delivery.

What should I do if freight arrives damaged?

Write the damage on the delivery receipt in specific terms before the driver leaves, have the driver sign next to it, photograph the freight on the truck, keep the packaging, and tell the carrier and the vendor in writing straight away. Time limits for claims are set by the carrier’s terms and the contract of carriage, so check them the same day.

How long should proof of delivery records be kept?

For as long as your company’s records policy and any contract require. They support invoice payment, warranty start dates and freight claims, so in practice they are kept with the purchase order file for the life of the equipment warranty at least.

This is a general-purpose template to help you lay a document out clearly. It is not legal advice, and it does not replace your company’s terms, your carrier’s tariff or the requirements of any regulation that applies to your shipment.
