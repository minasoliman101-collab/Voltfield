Bill of Lading Template (Straight, Truck Freight)

A straight bill of lading for truck freight, laid out the way carriers expect it. Fill it in here, then print it or save it as a PDF.

A bill of lading does three jobs at once: it is the carrier’s receipt for the freight, the contract of carriage, and the instruction that says who the freight is to be delivered to. A straight bill of lading, the kind used for almost all domestic truck freight, names one consignee and is not negotiable. The shipper prepares it, the driver signs it at pickup, and a copy travels with the load.

What goes in each field

BOL number
Your own reference for the shipment. The carrier will add its PRO number at pickup; record it when you get it, because that is the number the carrier tracks by.
Ship from and ship to
Full legal names and physical addresses, with a dock or gate and a phone number at each end. A site under construction often has no street number a truck can find, so give directions or coordinates in the special instructions.
Third-party bill to
Fill this in only when someone other than the shipper or the consignee pays the freight, for example a logistics provider. Leave it empty otherwise.
SCAC
The Standard Carrier Alpha Code, a two-to-four-letter code that identifies the carrier. Your carrier will tell you theirs.
Freight charge terms
Prepaid means the shipper pays the carrier. Collect means the consignee pays. Third party means the party named in the bill-to box pays. This should agree with the Incoterms rule or freight terms on the purchase order.
Handling units and pieces
A handling unit is what the forklift or crane picks up: a skid, a crate, a reel. Pieces are what is inside or on it. The driver signs for handling units, so that count is the one that has to be right.
Weight
Gross weight including the pallet or skid and packaging. For heavy equipment use the certified shipping weight from the manufacturer, since permits and axle loading are worked out from it.
HM
Mark this column for any line that is a regulated hazardous material. A hazmat line needs more than a mark: the proper shipping description, an emergency response number and a shipper’s certification, as the hazardous materials regulations require. This template does not cover that; use the carrier’s or your compliance team’s hazmat form. Lithium batteries are hazardous materials.
NMFC number and class
Used for less-than-truckload freight, where the rate depends on the freight class. A dedicated flatbed or heavy-haul move is usually priced by the load and these stay empty.
Declared value
A carrier’s liability is often limited to an amount per pound unless a higher value is declared and paid for. For a transformer the limited figure can be a small fraction of the replacement cost, so settle value and cargo insurance before the truck is loaded.
Signatures
The driver’s signature at pickup is the carrier’s receipt for the count and apparent condition. Keep the signed copy. At the other end, the consignee’s signature on the delivery receipt closes the loop: see the proof of delivery template.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Handling-unit counts that do not match what was loaded. The carrier is responsible for what the driver signed for, not what you meant.
  • Freight terms on the bill of lading that contradict the purchase order.
  • No delivery contact or appointment requirement for a site that cannot receive unannounced.
  • Leaving declared value empty on high-value equipment and discovering the liability limit after damage.
  • Shipping batteries or other regulated goods on a plain bill of lading with no hazardous materials description.

Questions

What is the difference between a straight and an order bill of lading?

A straight bill of lading names a specific consignee and is not negotiable: the carrier delivers to that party. An order bill of lading is negotiable and the freight is released to whoever holds the endorsed original, which is used where goods are sold or financed in transit. Domestic equipment shipments almost always move on a straight bill.

Who fills out the bill of lading?

Normally the shipper, since the shipper knows what was loaded. The carrier’s driver checks the count and apparent condition and signs it at pickup.

Is a bill of lading the same as a proof of delivery?

No. The bill of lading is created at pickup and shows what the carrier accepted. The proof of delivery is signed at the destination and shows what arrived and in what condition. A signed delivery copy of the bill of lading is often used as the proof of delivery.

Can I use this for ocean or air freight?

No. Ocean bills of lading and air waybills are issued by the carrier or forwarder in their own formats. This template is for domestic truck freight.

This is a general-purpose template to help you lay a document out clearly. It is not legal advice, and it does not replace your company’s terms, your carrier’s tariff or the requirements of any regulation that applies to your shipment.
