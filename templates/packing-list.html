Packing List Template for Equipment Shipments

What is in each crate, how big it is and what it weighs, with totals, and how it must be handled and stored. Fill it in here, then print it or save it as a PDF.

A packing list itemizes a shipment package by package: what each one contains, its dimensions and its weight. The carrier uses it to plan the load, customs uses it to check the goods, and the receiver uses it to find out whether everything arrived. For engineered equipment it does one more job. Large units ship in pieces, and the packing list is the only document that says which crate holds the bushings, the radiators or the control cabinet.

What goes in each field

Package number
Number packages “1 of 3”, “2 of 3” and mark the same on the package. The receiver then knows at a glance whether one is missing.
Contents
Tie every line to a purchase order line, and give serial or tag numbers for major items. For a crate of parts, list the parts and counts: “accessories” tells the receiver nothing when a relay is missing three months later.
Dimensions and weights
Net weight is the goods alone. Gross weight includes the skid, crate and packing, and is the figure the carrier and the crane need. State the units. For heavy pieces use certified weights, since permits and lift plans are based on them.
Totals
The quantity and weight columns add as you type. Total gross weight should match the bill of lading, and the package count should match its handling units.
Handling
Say where to lift and where not to, where the center of gravity is, and which way up. The rigging crew on site has usually never seen this unit before.
Storage
Equipment often sits for weeks before installation. State whether it can be outdoors, whether heaters need power, and how long it can stay in its shipping condition. Damage in storage is rarely covered by warranty.
Items shipped loose or separately
Anything not on this truck that the receiver might expect: parts following later, items shipped direct from a sub-supplier. This box prevents false shortage claims.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Describing a crate as “parts” or “accessories”.
  • Weights and package counts that disagree with the bill of lading.
  • No lifting or storage instructions for equipment that will wait in a laydown yard.
  • Leaving out items shipped separately, so the receiver reports them short.
  • Putting prices on the packing list. Those belong on the invoice.

Questions

What is a packing list?

A document that itemizes the contents of a shipment package by package, with quantities, dimensions and weights. It travels with the goods and is used by the carrier, by customs and by the receiver.

What is the difference between a packing list and a bill of lading?

The bill of lading is the contract with the carrier and its receipt for the freight, described in handling units. The packing list describes what is inside those units in detail. The two should agree on package count and gross weight.

What is the difference between a packing list and a commercial invoice?

The commercial invoice states what was sold and for how much, and is the basis for payment and for customs valuation. The packing list states how the goods are physically packed and normally carries no prices.

Is a packing list required?

For international shipments customs authorities generally expect one. For domestic freight it is not usually a legal requirement, but receiving a multi-package equipment shipment without one is very difficult.

This is a general-purpose template to help you lay a document out clearly. It is not legal advice, and it does not replace your company’s terms, your carrier’s tariff or the requirements of any regulation that applies to your shipment.
