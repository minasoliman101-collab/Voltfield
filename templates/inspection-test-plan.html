Inspection and Test Plan (ITP) Template

Every inspection and test in order, what counts as a pass, and whether the buyer holds, witnesses or reviews it. Fill it in here, then print it or save it as a PDF.

An inspection and test plan lists every inspection and test a piece of equipment goes through during manufacture, in sequence, with the acceptance criteria for each and the part each party plays. The vendor normally drafts it and the buyer marks where it wants to be present. Agreed before fabrication starts, it replaces a great many arguments later about what was supposed to be checked, to what standard, and whether anyone was told.

What goes in each field

Inspection or test
One line per activity, in the order it happens. The starting list here is generic. Replace it with the tests your governing standard and specification actually require; for the usual content by equipment type see FAT vs. SAT.
Procedure or standard
The document that says how the step is done: a clause of a standard, a specification section or a vendor procedure number. If nothing can be cited, nobody has agreed how the test is run.
Acceptance criteria
What a pass looks like, as a figure or a reference to where the figure is. “Satisfactory” is not a criterion. For guaranteed values such as losses and impedance, state the guarantee and the tolerance.
Vendor and buyer columns
Enter P, H, W or R for each party on each line. The vendor performs almost everything. The buyer chooses its level of involvement, and should choose sparingly: every hold point is a place where the buyer can stop the factory, and so a place where the buyer can cause delay.
Hold points
Reserve them for steps that cannot be inspected or undone afterwards, and for release for shipment. State the notice period and what happens if a hold point is passed without release.
Record
The piece of paper that proves the step was done. These records together make up the quality dossier delivered with the equipment.
Revision
An ITP changes as drawings are approved. Control it like a drawing, with a revision letter and both signatures on each issue.

Mistakes that cost money later

  • Agreeing the ITP after fabrication has started, when early inspection stages have already passed.
  • Acceptance criteria that do not state a value.
  • Making everything a hold point, then being unable to attend.
  • No notice period, so the invitation to witness arrives two days before the test.
  • Releasing for shipment by email with punch list items still open and unassigned.

Questions

What is an inspection and test plan?

A document listing each inspection and test carried out on a product during manufacture, in sequence, with the procedure, the acceptance criteria, the records produced and the involvement of each party at each step.

Who prepares the ITP?

The manufacturer normally drafts it, because it follows the manufacturer’s process. The buyer reviews it, adds its hold, witness and review points, and both parties approve it before fabrication.

What is the difference between a hold point and a witness point?

At a hold point the work must stop until the named party has attended or given written release. At a witness point the party is notified and may attend, but the work goes ahead if it does not.

Is an ITP the same as a quality plan?

No. A quality plan describes how the manufacturer’s quality system is applied to the order as a whole. The ITP is the specific list of inspections and tests for the product, and is often one section of the quality plan.

This is a general-purpose template to help you lay a document out clearly. It is not legal advice, and it does not replace your company’s terms, your carrier’s tariff or the requirements of any regulation that applies to your shipment.
