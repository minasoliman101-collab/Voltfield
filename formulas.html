Engineering Formulas for Power, Generation and Cooling

54 formulas on one page, each with its variables and what it assumes. Where there is a calculator that runs it, the link is on the card.

This is the page to keep open while reading a data sheet or checking someone else’s number. The formulas run from the circuit basics through distribution, motors, generation by turbine, wind, water and sun, battery storage and cooling. Constants that hide an assumption, such as 1.08 for air and 500 for water, say what they assume.

Circuit basics

Ohm’s law

V = I × R

V volts, I amps, R ohms

Rearranged: I = V / R and R = V / I.

Ohm’s law calculator →

Power in a DC or resistive circuit

P = V × I = I² × R = V² / R

P watts

The I²R form is the one that explains conductor heating and line losses.

Ohm’s law calculator →

Energy

E = P × t

E in kWh, P in kW, t in hours

Cost is energy times the rate: kWh × $/kWh.

Electricity cost calculator →

Conductor resistance

R = ρ × L / A

ρ resistivity, L length, A cross-section

Resistivity rises with temperature, so resistance tables state the temperature they are for.

Wire resistance calculator →

Resistors in series and parallel

Rs = R1 + R2    1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2

Two in parallel: Rp = R1R2 / (R1 + R2).

Reactance and impedance

XL = 2πfL    XC = 1 / (2πfC)    Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²)

f hertz, L henries, C farads, Z ohms

In an AC circuit Ohm’s law uses Z in place of R.

AC and three-phase power

Single-phase real power

P = V × I × PF

P watts, PF power factor

Watts to amps calculator →

Three-phase real power

P = √3 × VLL × I × PF

VLL line-to-line volts, I line amps

√3 is about 1.732.

Three-phase power calculator →

Three-phase apparent power

S = √3 × VLL × I

S in VA; divide by 1,000 for kVA

Equipment is rated in kVA because its heating depends on current, whatever the power factor.

Amps to kVA calculator →

Current from kW, three-phase

I = kW × 1000 / (√3 × VLL × PF)
kW to amps calculator →

Power triangle

S² = P² + Q²    PF = P / S

P kW, Q kVAR, S kVA

kW = kVA × PF.

kVA to kW calculator →

Power factor correction

Qc = P × (tan φ1 − tan φ2)

Qc kVAR of capacitors, φ = arccos(PF) before and after

Power factor calculator →

Line and phase voltage, wye

VLL = √3 × VLN

Which is why 480Y/277 V and 208Y/120 V come in pairs.

Delta vs. wye guide →

Conductors and protection

Voltage drop, single-phase

VD = 2 × I × R × L / 1000

R ohms per 1,000 ft, L one-way feet

The 2 accounts for the return conductor.

Voltage drop calculator →

Voltage drop, three-phase

VD = √3 × I × R × L / 1000

R ohms per 1,000 ft, L one-way feet

This resistance-only form is close for small conductors. Large conductors need reactance included.

Voltage drop calculator →

Percent voltage drop

%VD = VD / Vsource × 100

Common design targets are 3% on a branch or feeder and 5% overall.

Voltage drop calculator →

Continuous load

OCPD ≥ 1.25 × Icontinuous + Inoncontinuous

A continuous load runs three hours or more

The same thing seen from the other side: a standard breaker carries 80% of its rating continuously.

Breaker size calculator →

Corrected ampacity

Iallowed = Itable × Ftemperature × Fbundling

Factors from the adopted electrical code

Ampacity calculator →

Conduit fill

Fill % = Σ conductor area / conduit internal area × 100

Areas include insulation

Conduit fill calculator →

Voltage unbalance

% unbalance = max deviation from average / average × 100

Using the three line-to-line voltages

The NEMA definition. A few percent of unbalance causes a much larger current unbalance in a motor.

Voltage unbalance calculator →

Transformers and fault current

Full-load current, three-phase

IFL = kVA × 1000 / (√3 × VLL)

Single-phase: I = kVA × 1000 / V.

kVA to amps calculator →

Turns ratio

Vp / Vs = Np / Ns = Is / Ip

p primary, s secondary, N turns

Voltage goes with turns, current goes against them.

kVA needed for a load

kVA = kW / PF

Then add margin and round up to a standard rating.

Transformer sizing calculator →

Fault current at the secondary

ISC = IFL × 100 / %Z

%Z nameplate impedance

Assumes an infinite source, so it is the upper bound. A 5.75% unit lets through about 17 times full-load current.

Fault current calculator →

Efficiency

η = Pout / (Pout + Pno-load + Pload)

Load loss scales with the square of loading

Peak efficiency falls where load loss equals no-load loss.

Motors, pumps and fans

Synchronous speed

ns = 120 × f / p

n rpm, f hertz, p number of poles

A 4-pole motor on 60 Hz: 1,800 rpm.

Motor speed calculator →

Slip

s = (ns − n) / ns

n actual shaft speed

Torque

T = 5252 × hp / rpm

T in lb·ft

Metric: T (N·m) = 9550 × kW / rpm.

Motor torque calculator →

Horsepower to kilowatts

kW = hp × 0.746

That is shaft power. Electrical input is shaft power divided by efficiency.

hp to kW calculator →

Motor input current, three-phase

I = hp × 746 / (√3 × V × η × PF)

η efficiency as a decimal

For sizing conductors and protection, codes use table currents, not this figure.

Affinity laws

Q ∝ N    H ∝ N²    P ∝ N³

Q flow, H head or pressure, P power, N speed

At 80% speed a fan or pump draws about half the power. Static head weakens the cube law.

Affinity laws calculator →

Pump power

bhp = GPM × head × SG / (3960 × η)

head in feet, SG specific gravity, η pump efficiency

Pump power calculator →

Pipe velocity

v = 0.4085 × GPM / d²

v ft/s, d inside diameter in inches

Pipe velocity calculator →

Power generation

Capacity factor

CF = energy produced / (rated power × hours in the period)

A 100 MW plant that makes 350,400 MWh in a year ran at a 40% capacity factor.

Heat rate and efficiency

η = 3412 / heat rate

Heat rate in Btu per kWh

A heat rate of 6,824 Btu/kWh is 50% efficient. Lower is better.

Fuel input

MMBtu/h = MW × heat rate / 1000

Heat rate in Btu per kWh

Generator frequency

f = n × p / 120

n rpm, p poles

A 2-pole machine makes 60 Hz at 3,600 rpm, a 4-pole machine at 1,800 rpm.

Speed and poles calculator →

Generator runtime

hours = usable fuel / fuel burn rate

Burn rate at the actual load, not at nameplate

Generator runtime calculator →

Wind turbine power

P = ½ × ρ × A × v³ × Cp

ρ air density (about 1.225 kg/m³), A swept area m², v wind speed m/s

Power goes with the cube of wind speed. Cp cannot exceed the Betz limit of 0.593.

Hydro power

P = η × ρ × g × Q × H

ρ 1,000 kg/m³, g 9.81 m/s², Q flow m³/s, H net head m

In kW: about 9.81 × η × Q × H.

Solar array output

P = PSTC × (G / 1000) × [1 + γ × (Tcell − 25)]

G irradiance W/m², γ power temperature coefficient per °C (negative)

Before inverter, wiring and soiling losses.

Maximum string voltage

Vmax = N × Voc × [1 + β × (Tmin − 25)]

N modules in series, β Voc temperature coefficient per °C (negative)

Voltage is highest on the coldest morning, which sets the string length.

Solar string calculator →

DC/AC ratio

ILR = PDC, array / PAC, inverter

Also called inverter loading ratio.

DC/AC ratio calculator →

Levelized cost of energy

LCOE = Σ [costt / (1 + r)t] / Σ [energyt / (1 + r)t]

r discount rate, t year

Costs are capital, fuel and O&M over the plant life.

Battery storage

Amp-hours to energy

kWh = Ah × V / 1000

V nominal voltage

C-rate

C = P / E

P power in kW, E energy in kWh

Duration at full power is 1 / C. A 0.25C system is a four-hour system.

C-rate guide →

Runtime

t = E × DoD × η / P

DoD usable depth of discharge, η discharge efficiency

Round-trip efficiency

RTE = energy out / energy in

Measured at the same point, AC to AC

Nameplate energy for a required delivery

Enameplate = Erequired / (DoD × η × end-of-life fraction)

Capacity fades, so the day-one system is larger than the contract figure or is augmented later.

Cooling and data center

Electrical load to heat

BTU/h = W × 3.412

Essentially all power drawn by IT equipment ends up as heat in the room.

BTU to tons calculator →

Tons of refrigeration

tons = BTU/h / 12,000

1 ton = 3.517 kW of heat

BTU to tons calculator →

Airflow for a heat load

CFM = BTU/h / (1.08 × ΔT)

ΔT air temperature rise in °F

1.08 is for standard air at sea level. Per kW that is about 3,160 / ΔT.

Airflow (CFM) calculator →

Chilled water flow

GPM = BTU/h / (500 × ΔT)

ΔT water temperature rise in °F

500 is for plain water. From tons: GPM = tons × 24 / ΔT.

PUE

PUE = total facility energy / IT equipment energy

1.0 would mean every watt reaches the IT load.

PUE calculator →

Using these

  • Check the units before the arithmetic. Most wrong answers come from kW where kVA was meant, line-to-neutral voltage where line-to-line was meant, or a one-way length where the formula wants the round trip.
  • A formula gives a number, a code gives a requirement. Conductor and breaker sizes are set by the adopted electrical code and its tables. These relations explain the tables; they do not replace them.
  • Want the whole range at once? The reference charts tabulate several of these, and the practice questions work them with numbers.
