Substation Single-Line Diagram: A Typical Arrangement

A single-transformer distribution substation, drawn from the incoming line down to the feeders, with each element explained.

A typical arrangement for learning. It carries no ratings on purpose and is not a design. Symbols are explained in the legend.

Reading it from the top

Transmission line
The source. Power arrives at transmission or subtransmission voltage.
Line disconnect switch
Gives a visible break so the station can be isolated from the line. It is operated only when no current is flowing.
High-voltage circuit breaker
Interrupts load and fault current on the high side. On drawings it carries ANSI device number 52. See ANSI device numbers.
Transformer disconnect
With the line disconnect, isolates the breaker on both sides so it can be maintained.
Power transformer
Steps the voltage down. Surge arresters sit close to its terminals, and a tap changer adjusts the ratio to hold secondary voltage.
Main secondary breaker
Protects the bus and lets the transformer be separated from it.
Medium-voltage bus
The common connection for all feeders. A fault here takes out the whole station, which is why larger stations split it.
Feeder breakers
One per outgoing circuit, each with its own overcurrent protection, so a fault on one feeder does not interrupt the others.
Station service
A small transformer that powers the control house, the battery charger, lighting and heaters. The battery is what trips the breakers if AC is lost.

What a real single-line adds

A single-line diagram for construction is a controlled document that an engineer is responsible for. Compared with this one it would also show:

  • Voltage, MVA, impedance and connection for the transformer, and continuous and interrupting ratings for every breaker
  • Current and voltage transformers with their ratios, and the relays they feed, by device number
  • Revenue metering
  • Grounding of the transformer neutral
  • A second transformer and a bus tie, in any station that must ride through the loss of one unit
  • Capacitor banks or voltage regulators on the bus
