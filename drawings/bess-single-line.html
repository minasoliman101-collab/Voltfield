Battery Energy Storage (BESS) Single-Line Diagram: A Typical Arrangement

A grid-connected battery plant drawn from the interconnection down to the cells, including the auxiliary power it cannot run without.

A typical arrangement for learning. It carries no ratings on purpose and is not a design. Symbols are explained in the legend.

Reading it from the top

Point of interconnection
Power flows both ways here. A storage plant is a load when charging and a generator when discharging, and it is studied as both.
Main power transformer
Sized for the plant’s power rating in MW, not its energy in MWh. Duration changes the number of battery containers, not the transformer.
Medium-voltage bus
Collects the blocks. Larger plants split it into sections.
Pad-mounted transformer
Matches the converter’s AC voltage to the collector voltage. Frequently one transformer serves two converters.
Power conversion system
A bidirectional inverter: it rectifies to charge and inverts to discharge. Whether it is grid-following or grid-forming decides what services the plant can provide.
DC disconnect and fuses
Isolate each battery container from the converter and clear DC faults, which do not have the current zeros that help an AC breaker.
Battery
Racks of modules in series and parallel, supervised by a battery management system. Power against energy is the C-rate.
Auxiliary transformer and loads
Cooling, controls, and fire detection and suppression. These loads are large, run in every state and count against round-trip efficiency. Many sites give them a backup supply.

What a real single-line adds

A single-line diagram for construction is a controlled document that an engineer is responsible for. Compared with this one it would also show:

  • Power and energy ratings, voltages and impedances
  • Protection on both the AC and DC sides, including ground-fault detection on the DC bus
  • The plant controller and energy management system
  • Backup supply to the auxiliary bus so that cooling and fire systems survive a grid outage
  • Fire alarm and emergency shutdown interfaces required by NFPA 855
  • Any co-located solar, which may share the transformer and the interconnection
