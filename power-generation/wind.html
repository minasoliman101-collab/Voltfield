Wind Power Plants: Turbines, Collector Systems and Substations

Why wind speed matters three times over, what is inside a turbine and between the turbines and the grid, and why the blades decide the delivery plan.

A wind turbine converts the kinetic energy of moving air into shaft power, and a generator converts that into electricity. The power available rises with the cube of the wind speed, which is the single most important fact in wind energy. Double the wind speed and there is eight times the power in it. That is why siting and hub height matter so much, and why a turbine spends most of its life well below its rating.

How it makes power

P = ½ × ρ × A × v³ × Cp

Here ρ is air density, about 1.225 kg/m³ at sea level, A is the area swept by the rotor in m², v is wind speed in m/s, and Cp is the fraction of the wind’s power the rotor captures. Cp cannot exceed 0.593, the Betz limit, and good modern rotors reach somewhat under 0.5.

A rotor 150 m in diameter sweeps 17,671 m². In an 8 m/s wind, with Cp of 0.45, it captures 0.5 × 1.225 × 17,671 × 512 × 0.45, about 2.5 MW. At 10 m/s the same rotor would capture nearly twice that.

Because swept area goes with the square of the diameter, a longer blade pays twice. Because of the cube law, a small error in the wind resource estimate becomes a large error in energy.

Numbers worth knowing

  • Cut-in wind speed is roughly 3 to 4 m/s. Below it the turbine does not generate.
  • Rated wind speed is roughly 11 to 13 m/s. Above it, pitch control holds output at the rating.
  • Cut-out wind speed is around 25 m/s, where the turbine shuts down to protect itself.
  • Capacity factor for onshore wind commonly falls between about 25% and 45% depending on the site and the machine. A 3 MW turbine at 35% produces about 9,200 MWh a year.
  • Air density matters. Cold, dense air at sea level carries more power than warm air at altitude at the same speed.

Buying and shipping it

Turbines are bought under a turbine supply agreement, usually with a long-term service agreement and a guaranteed power curve and availability. The buyer’s balance-of-plant scope is the foundations, collector system, substation and interconnection. Logistics shape everything: blades tens of meters long and tower sections at the limit of road clearances need route surveys, permits, escorts and sometimes road modifications, and deliveries have to be sequenced to the crane. A blade damaged in transit is expensive and slow to replace, so receiving inspection and clear records at handover matter more here than almost anywhere.

Questions

How is wind turbine power calculated?

Power equals one half times air density times rotor swept area times wind speed cubed times the power coefficient. With density in kg per cubic meter, area in square meters and speed in meters per second, the result is in watts.

What is the Betz limit?

The theoretical maximum fraction of the wind’s kinetic energy a rotor can extract, 16/27 or about 59.3 percent. Real turbines achieve somewhat less than 50 percent.

What is the difference between a Type 3 and a Type 4 wind turbine?

A Type 3 turbine uses a doubly-fed induction generator: the stator connects directly to the grid and a converter rated at roughly a third of the machine feeds the rotor. A Type 4 turbine passes all of its power through a full-scale converter, which decouples the generator from the grid.
