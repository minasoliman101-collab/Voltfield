Gas Turbine and Combined-Cycle Power Plants: Equipment Reference

How a gas plant turns fuel into megawatts, what the main equipment is and what a specification has to pin down, and why a turbine makes less power on a hot afternoon.

A gas turbine burns fuel in compressed air and expands the hot gas through a turbine that drives a generator. Run on its own that is a simple-cycle plant. Add a boiler on the exhaust and a steam turbine, and it is a combined-cycle plant that gets roughly half as much power again from the same fuel. Simple-cycle units start fast and are used for peaking. Combined-cycle plants are more efficient and run for longer hours.

How it makes power

The measure that matters is heat rate: the fuel energy needed to make one kilowatt-hour, in Btu per kWh. Efficiency follows directly from it.

η = 3412 / heat rate

A heat rate of 9,750 Btu/kWh is 35% efficient. A heat rate of 6,200 is 55%. Modern simple-cycle machines fall broadly between 35% and 42%, and combined-cycle plants between about 55% and a little over 60%, on a lower-heating-value basis. Always check whether a quoted heat rate is on a lower or a higher heating value basis: for natural gas the two differ by about 10%, and comparing one bidder’s LHV figure with another’s HHV figure is a classic error.

Fuel input (MMBtu/h) = MW × heat rate / 1000

A 250 MW block at 6,400 Btu/kWh burns 1,600 MMBtu of gas an hour.

Numbers worth knowing

  • Ratings are quoted at ISO conditions: 15°C (59°F), sea level, 60% relative humidity. A site is rarely at ISO conditions.
  • Output falls as ambient temperature rises, because hot air is less dense and the compressor moves less mass. A rough guide is a fraction of a percent per degree Fahrenheit, which adds up to a double-digit loss on a very hot day. Inlet cooling recovers some of it.
  • Output falls with altitude for the same reason, by roughly 3% to 4% per 1,000 ft.
  • Part-load efficiency is worse than full-load efficiency. A heat rate guarantee is for a stated load point.
  • The plant consumes some of its own output. Net output is gross output less auxiliary load, and guarantees should say which is meant.

Buying and shipping it

The turbine, the GSU transformer and the HRSG are the long-lead items and set the project schedule, so they are often ordered before the rest of the design is complete. Performance is bought on guarantees: output and heat rate at defined site conditions, proven by a performance test with agreed correction curves, with damages for a shortfall. Most owners also sign a long-term service agreement covering scheduled maintenance of the hot gas path, which over the life of the plant can cost as much as the machine. Transport is a project in itself: turbines, generators and GSUs are among the heaviest loads that move by road and rail.

Questions

What is the difference between simple cycle and combined cycle?

A simple-cycle plant is a gas turbine driving a generator, with the exhaust heat released to the air. A combined-cycle plant captures that exhaust heat in a heat recovery steam generator and uses the steam to drive a second, steam turbine, which raises efficiency from roughly 35 to 42 percent to roughly 55 to 60 percent or a little more.

What is heat rate?

The fuel energy a plant uses to produce one kilowatt-hour of electricity, usually in Btu per kWh. Lower is better. Efficiency equals 3,412 divided by the heat rate.

Why does a gas turbine produce less power on a hot day?

A gas turbine moves a fixed volume of air. Hot air is less dense, so less mass flows through the machine and less fuel can be burned, which reduces output and worsens heat rate. High altitude has the same effect.
