Transmission and Substations: Equipment Reference

Every generator and every large load meets the grid at a substation. This is what is inside the fence, why it is arranged the way it is, and what a specification has to say.

Transmission moves bulk power at high voltage, and substations are where voltage is changed, circuits are switched and faults are cleared. A generating plant, a data center campus and a battery project all connect through one, and the substation is frequently the item that sets the project schedule, since it holds the equipment with the longest lead times.

How it makes power

The reason for high voltage is in two formulas. Power delivered on a three-phase line is

P = √3 × V × I × PF

and the power lost as heat in the conductors is

Ploss = 3 × I² × R

For the same power, doubling the voltage halves the current, and halving the current cuts the loss to a quarter. Moving 100 MW at 0.95 power factor takes about 4,400 A at 13.8 kV and about 440 A at 138 kV. The higher voltage needs taller structures and more insulation, and that cost is what is traded against losses when a transmission voltage is chosen.

Numbers worth knowing

  • Voltage classes in North America: distribution up to about 35 kV, subtransmission 46 to 69 kV, transmission 115 kV to 345 kV, and extra-high voltage at 500 kV and 765 kV.
  • Bus arrangements trade cost against reliability. A single bus is cheapest, and a bus fault or breaker maintenance takes everything out. A ring bus gives each circuit two breakers with one breaker per circuit overall. Breaker-and-a-half uses three breakers for every two circuits and is standard at large transmission stations. Double bus, double breaker is the most reliable and the most expensive.
  • Transformer impedance sets fault current. Available fault current on the secondary is about the full-load current times 100 divided by the percent impedance.
  • Air-insulated or gas-insulated. Gas-insulated switchgear takes a fraction of the land of an air-insulated yard and costs considerably more, so it is used where space is tight.
  • HVDC is used for very long lines, submarine cables and ties between grids that are not synchronized.

Buying and shipping it

Large power transformers and high-voltage breakers are the long-lead items, and a substation schedule is built backward from their delivery dates. Transformers are evaluated on total owning cost, not price, with losses capitalized. The interconnecting utility’s standards usually govern ratings, relay types and approved manufacturers, so obtain them before writing a specification. A large transformer ships partly disassembled and without oil, with an impact recorder, on specialized rail or road equipment under permit; it is then assembled, vacuum-filled and tested on site, which is a scope someone has to own in the order.

Questions

Why is electricity transmitted at high voltage?

For a given power, higher voltage means lower current, and line losses rise with the square of the current. Doubling the voltage halves the current and cuts the resistive loss to a quarter, which makes long-distance transmission economic.

What is the difference between a circuit breaker and a disconnect switch?

A circuit breaker can interrupt load current and fault current. A disconnect switch cannot: it is opened only after the breaker has interrupted the current, to give a visible isolating gap so equipment can be worked on safely.

What is a breaker-and-a-half scheme?

A substation bus arrangement with two main buses and three breakers in series between them for every two circuits, so each circuit is served by one and a half breakers. Any breaker or either bus can be taken out of service without interrupting a circuit.
