Hydropower Plants: Turbines, Generators and Electrical Equipment

Power from falling water comes down to two numbers, head and flow. They decide the turbine, the generator speed and most of what follows.

A hydro plant converts the potential energy of water at a height into shaft power in a turbine, and then into electricity in a generator. The power depends on how far the water falls, the head, and how much of it flows. Hydro units are efficient, last for generations and can change output in seconds, which makes them valuable for balancing the grid far beyond their share of energy.

How it makes power

P = η × ρ × g × Q × H

With water density ρ of 1,000 kg/m³ and g of 9.81 m/s², flow Q in m³/s and net head H in meters, this simplifies to a form worth memorizing:

P (kW) ≈ 9.81 × η × Q × H

A plant with 50 m³/s of flow and 100 m of net head, at an overall efficiency of 90%, produces 9.81 × 0.90 × 50 × 100, about 44,100 kW, or 44 MW. Net head is the gross height difference less the friction losses in the intake, tunnel and penstock, so pipe sizing is part of the power calculation.

Numbers worth knowing

  • Speed follows from frequency and poles: n = 120 × f / p. A 60 Hz generator with 48 poles turns at 150 rpm. Slow turbines need many poles, which is why hydro generators are wide and short.
  • Turbine efficiency at the design point is commonly above 90%. Overall plant efficiency, water to wire, is somewhat lower.
  • Capacity factor depends on water, not on the machine. Run-of-river plants follow the river; storage plants are dispatched.
  • Pumped storage uses reversible pump-turbines to move water uphill when power is cheap and generate when it is needed. Round-trip efficiency is typically around 70% to 80%.
  • Service life is long. Civil works last a century or more, and generating equipment is commonly refurbished after several decades. Much of today’s hydro procurement is rehabilitation.

Buying and shipping it

Hydro equipment is custom. Each runner is designed for one site’s head and flow, often proven with a model test before manufacture, and efficiency is guaranteed and verified by a field test. Lead times are long and the supplier base is small. Rehabilitation work adds its own difficulty: new equipment has to fit embedded parts and concrete that are decades old, drawings may be incomplete, and the outage window is fixed by the river. Sites are frequently remote, with access roads, tunnels and powerhouse cranes that set hard limits on the size and weight of anything delivered, so shipping dimensions belong in the specification from the start.

Questions

How is hydropower calculated?

Power equals efficiency times water density times gravitational acceleration times flow times net head. In practical units, kilowatts are about 9.81 times efficiency times flow in cubic meters per second times head in meters.

What is the difference between Pelton, Francis and Kaplan turbines?

They suit different heads. A Pelton is an impulse turbine for high head and low flow. A Francis is a reaction turbine for medium head and is the most widely used. A Kaplan is a propeller turbine with adjustable blades for low head and high flow.

What is pumped storage hydropower?

A plant with an upper and a lower reservoir and reversible pump-turbines. It pumps water uphill using surplus electricity and releases it through the turbines to generate when demand is high, returning roughly 70 to 80 percent of the energy used.
