Ohm's Law and Power: Practice Questions

Voltage, current, resistance, power and energy on DC and resistive AC circuits.

Eight questions on the relationships everything else is built from: V = I × R, P = V × I, and what follows when one of them changes. No calculator is needed for most of them, but the working matters more than the answer.

Question 1

A resistor of 8 Ω is connected across a 120 V supply. What current flows?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 15.0 A

Ohm's law: I = V / R = 120 / 8 = 15.0 A. Multiplying voltage by resistance, or dividing the wrong way round, are the two usual slips.

Question 2

A current of 2 A flows through a 30 Ω resistor. How much power does the resistor dissipate?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 120 W

P = I² × R = 2² × 30 = 120 W. The answer 60 is I × R, which is the voltage across the resistor, not the power.

Related: Ohm's Law Calculator

Question 3

A 1,500 W heater is connected to a 120 V circuit. What current does it draw?

Choices for question 3
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 12.5 A

I = P / V = 1,500 / 120 = 12.5 A. A heater is a resistive load, so no power factor is involved.

Related: Watts to Amps Calculator

Question 4

A heating element is rated 2,000 W at 240 V. Its resistance does not change. What power does it produce on a 120 V supply?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 500 W

Power goes with the square of the voltage when resistance is fixed: P = V² / R. Halving the voltage gives one quarter of the power, 2,000 / 4 = 500 W. Halving the power is the common wrong answer.

Related: Ohm's Law Calculator

Question 5

What is the resistance of a 1,500 W, 120 V heating element at its operating temperature?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 9.6 Ω

R = V² / P = 120² / 1,500 = 14,400 / 1,500 = 9.6 Ω. The figure 12.5 is the current in amps, not the resistance.

Related: Ohm's Law Calculator

Question 6

A two-wire 120 V circuit runs 100 ft, one way, in 12 AWG copper (6,530 circular mils) and carries 16 A. Using K = 12.9, what is the voltage drop?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 6.32 V

The current travels out and back, so the conductor length is 200 ft. VD = 2 × K × I × L / cmil = 2 × 12.9 × 16 × 100 / 6,530 = 6.32 V, which is 5.3 percent of 120 V. Using the one-way length alone gives half the true figure.

Related: Voltage Drop Calculator

Question 7

A 10 kW load runs 24 hours a day. At an energy rate of $0.12 per kWh, what does it cost per day?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. $28.80

Energy = 10 kW × 24 h = 240 kWh. Cost = 240 × 0.12 = $28.80 per day, or about $10,512 over a year.

Related: Electricity Cost Calculator

Question 8

On an AC circuit with a power factor below 1.0, which statement is true?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. The volt-amperes are greater than the watts.

Volt-amperes are voltage times current. Watts are that product multiplied by the power factor, so with a power factor below one the watts are always the smaller number. The two are equal only at unity power factor.
