Energy, Demand and Efficiency: Practice Questions

Kilowatt-hours, demand charges, load factor, UPS losses and what they cost over a year.

Power is a rate and energy is that rate held over time, and an electricity bill charges for both: the kilowatt-hours used and the highest kilowatts drawn. These questions keep the two apart, then look at what a few percent of loss costs when it runs all year.

Question 1

A load draws a steady 75 kW around the clock. How much energy does it use in a 30-day month?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 54,000 kWh

Hours = 24 × 30 = 720. Energy = 75 kW × 720 h = 54,000 kWh.

Question 2

A facility uses 200,000 kWh in a month with a peak demand of 500 kW. Energy costs $0.08 per kWh and demand costs $15 per kW. What is the bill, before taxes and fixed charges?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. $23,500

Energy charge = 200,000 × 0.08 = $16,000. Demand charge = 500 × 15 = $7,500. Total = $23,500. The demand charge is nearly a third of the bill, and it is set by the single highest interval in the month.

Question 3

A facility used 200,000 kWh in a 720-hour month, with a peak demand of 500 kW. What was its load factor?

Choices for question 3
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 0.56

Load factor = energy used / (peak demand × hours) = 200,000 / (500 × 720) = 0.56. On average the facility ran at about 56 percent of its peak. A higher load factor spreads the demand charge over more kilowatt-hours.

Question 4

A UPS supplies a 500 kW load at an efficiency of 96 percent. How much power is lost in the UPS?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 20.8 kW

Input = 500 / 0.96 = 520.8 kW. Loss = input − output = 520.8 − 500 = 20.8 kW. Efficiency is output divided by input, so the loss is 4 percent of the input, not of the output.

Question 5

A UPS loses 20.8 kW continuously. At $0.10 per kWh, what does that loss cost over a year?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. $18,250

Energy = 20.8 kW × 8,760 h = 182,500 kWh. Cost = 182,500 × 0.10 = $18,250 a year, before counting the cooling needed to remove that heat.

Question 6

A data center has an IT load of 1,000 kW and a PUE of 1.4. How much energy does the whole facility use in a year?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 12,264,000 kWh

Total power = IT power × PUE = 1,000 × 1.4 = 1,400 kW. Energy = 1,400 × 8,760 = 12,264,000 kWh.

Question 7

A UPS loses 20.8 kW as heat. What is that in BTU per hour?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 71,086 BTU/h

BTU/h = kW × 3,412 = 20.8 × 3,412 = 71,086 BTU/h, a little under six tons of cooling. Every kilowatt lost in the electrical system has to be removed by the mechanical one.

Question 8

Two UPS systems carry the same load. One is 94 percent efficient and the other 97 percent. How do their losses compare?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. The 94 percent unit loses about twice as much.

Loss is roughly 100 minus the efficiency: about 6 percent against about 3 percent of the power passing through. Three points of efficiency sounds small, but it halves the loss, and the heat that has to be cooled away.
