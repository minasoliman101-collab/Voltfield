Ground-Fault Protection of Equipment: Practice Questions

When NEC 230.95 requires GFPE, how the disconnect rating is judged, the maximum settings, the exceptions and the on-site test.

Ground-fault protection of equipment is a short rule that produces a lot of exam questions, because almost every word in it can be tested: the system type, the voltage to ground, the ampere rating and how that rating is judged. These ten questions cover each of those in turn. They follow the wording of the 2023 National Electrical Code.

Question 1

Which of these services requires ground-fault protection of equipment under NEC 230.95?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. A 480Y/277 V solidly grounded wye service with a 1,200 A service disconnect.

Three conditions must all be met: a solidly grounded wye system, more than 150 volts to ground (and not more than 1,000 volts phase-to-phase), and a service disconnect rated 1,000 amperes or more. Only the 480Y/277 V, 1,200 A service meets all three.

Question 2

At what service disconnect rating does NEC 230.95 begin to require ground-fault protection of equipment?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 1,000 amperes or more

The requirement applies to each service disconnecting means rated 1,000 amperes or more, on a solidly grounded wye service of more than 150 volts to ground.

Question 3

What is the maximum setting permitted for the ground-fault protection required by NEC 230.95?

Choices for question 3
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 1,200 amperes

The maximum setting of the ground-fault protection is 1,200 amperes. Lower settings are allowed and are often needed to coordinate with devices downstream.

Question 4

For ground-fault currents of 3,000 amperes or more, what is the maximum time delay NEC 230.95 permits?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. One second

The maximum time delay is one second for ground-fault currents equal to or greater than 3,000 amperes. An arcing ground fault does a great deal of damage in that time, which is why shorter delays are normally set where coordination allows.

Question 5

A 480Y/277 V service has a 1,200 A fused switch as its service disconnect, fitted with 800 A fuses. Is ground-fault protection of equipment required?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. Yes, because the rating is that of the largest fuse the switch can accept.

The rating of the service disconnect is taken as the rating of the largest fuse that can be installed. A 1,200 A switch can accept 1,200 A fuses, so it is a 1,200 A disconnect for this rule and protection is required, whatever fuses are fitted today.

Question 6

A service disconnect is a circuit breaker whose continuous-current trip can be adjusted up to 1,200 A. It is set to 800 A. On a 480Y/277 V service, how is its rating judged for NEC 230.95?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. As 1,200 A, the highest setting to which the trip can be adjusted.

For a circuit breaker the rating is the highest continuous-current trip setting for which the installed overcurrent device is rated or can be adjusted. A breaker that can be turned up to 1,200 A is a 1,200 A disconnect, so ground-fault protection is required.

Question 7

To which of these does the ground-fault protection requirement of NEC 230.95 NOT apply?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. A service disconnect for a continuous industrial process where a non-orderly shutdown would introduce additional or increased hazards.

The exception covers a service disconnect for a continuous industrial process where a non-orderly shutdown will introduce additional or increased hazards. In that case tripping on a ground fault could be more dangerous than the fault.

Question 8

When must the ground-fault protection system required by NEC 230.95 be performance tested?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. When it is first installed on site, with a written record kept.

The system must be performance tested when first installed on site, in accordance with the instructions provided with the equipment, and a written record of the test must be made available to the authority having jurisdiction. Wiring errors, especially at the neutral sensor, are normally found by this test.

Question 9

How does ground-fault protection of equipment (GFPE) differ from a ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI)?

Choices for question 9
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. GFPE protects equipment and is set in hundreds of amperes; a GFCI protects people and trips at a few milliamperes.

A Class A GFCI is a personnel protection device that trips at about 5 milliamperes. GFPE exists to limit damage to equipment from arcing ground faults, with pickups that may be as high as 1,200 amperes. Neither can do the other's job.

Question 10

In a health care facility, where ground-fault protection is provided on the service disconnect, what else does the NEC require?

Choices for question 10
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. An additional step of ground-fault protection on the next level of feeder disconnects, under 517.17.

NEC 517.17 requires an additional step of ground-fault protection in the next level of feeder disconnecting means downstream toward the load, and the two levels must be selective. A ground fault on a feeder then opens the feeder device and leaves the rest of the facility energized.
