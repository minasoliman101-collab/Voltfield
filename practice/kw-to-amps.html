kW to Amps: Practice Questions

Converting kilowatts to amperes on single-phase, three-phase and DC circuits, with and without power factor.

Kilowatts cannot be turned into amperes without knowing three things: the voltage, whether the circuit is single-phase or three-phase, and the power factor. Each question gives all three. The wrong choices are what you get by dropping one of them.

Question 1

A motor draws 18.5 kW from a 400 V three-phase supply at a power factor of 0.85. What is the line current?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 31.4 A

I = kW × 1,000 / (1.732 × V × PF) = 18,500 / (1.732 × 400 × 0.85) = 31.4 A.

Question 2

A pump motor draws 2.2 kW from a 240 V single-phase supply at a power factor of 0.8. What current does it draw?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 11.5 A

I = kW × 1,000 / (V × PF) = 2,200 / (240 × 0.8) = 11.5 A.

Question 4

A resistance heater is rated 10 kW at 240 V, single-phase. What current does it draw?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 41.7 A

A resistance heater has a power factor of 1.0, so I = W / V = 10,000 / 240 = 41.7 A.

Question 8

A DC load draws 5 kW from a 48 V battery system. What current flows?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 104.2 A

On DC there is no power factor and no 1.732: I = W / V = 5,000 / 48 = 104.2 A. Low-voltage DC systems carry large currents for modest power, which is why their conductors are so heavy.
