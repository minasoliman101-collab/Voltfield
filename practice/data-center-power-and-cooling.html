Data Center Power and Cooling: Practice Questions

PUE, redundancy, cooling load in tons, airflow per kW and chilled water flow.

Almost all the electricity an IT load draws becomes heat, which is why the electrical and mechanical sides of a data center are sized from the same number. These questions move between the two.

Question 1

A data center draws 1,500 kW in total, of which 1,000 kW reaches the IT equipment. What is its PUE?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 1.50

PUE = total facility power / IT power = 1,500 / 1,000 = 1.50. The inverse, 0.67, is the older DCiE metric expressed as a fraction.

Question 2

In that facility the overhead is 500 kW. If it holds for a whole year, how much energy is that?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 4,380,000 kWh

A year has 8,760 hours. 500 kW × 8,760 h = 4,380,000 kWh.

Question 3

How many tons of sensible cooling does a 200 kW IT load require, before any redundancy?

Choices for question 3
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 56.9 tons

BTU/h = 200 × 3,412 = 682,428. Tons = BTU/h / 12,000 = 56.9 tons. One ton of cooling is 3.517 kW, so dividing kW by 3.517 gives the same answer.

Question 4

A rack dissipates 10 kW, and the air passing through it rises by 20°F. What airflow is needed?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 1,580 CFM

CFM = BTU/h / (1.08 × ΔT) = 34,121 / (1.08 × 20) = 1,580 CFM, or about 158 CFM per kW. Leaving out the 1.08 gives 1,706 CFM.

Question 5

A 100 ton cooling load is served by chilled water with a 10°F difference between supply and return. What flow is needed?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 240 GPM

GPM = tons × 24 / ΔT = 100 × 24 / 10 = 240 GPM, which is the familiar 2.4 GPM per ton. A wider temperature difference needs less flow.

Question 6

A cooling load needs three chillers to carry it. How many chillers does a 2N design install?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 6

N is three. 2N is two complete, independent systems of N, so six chillers. Four would be N+1 and five would be N+2.

Related: N+1 vs. 2N Redundancy

Question 7

With hot aisle containment, what is the condition of the open room outside the contained aisle?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. Cool: the room is the supply air path.

Hot aisle containment encloses the exhaust side of the racks and ducts that air back to the cooling units, leaving the room filled with supply air. Cold aisle containment is the reverse: the cold aisle is enclosed and the room runs hot.

Related: Hot Aisle vs. Cold Aisle Containment

Question 8

What is the essential difference between a CRAC and a CRAH?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. A CRAC has its own compressor; a CRAH uses chilled water from a separate plant.

A computer room air conditioner is a complete refrigeration machine in a cabinet. A computer room air handler has only a coil, fans and a valve, and depends on a chiller plant for its cooling.
