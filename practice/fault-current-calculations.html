Fault Current Calculations: Practice Questions

Available fault current from transformer kVA and impedance, the infinite-bus method, and comparing it with equipment ratings.

The quickest estimate of available fault current assumes the supply ahead of the transformer is unlimited, so the transformer impedance is the only thing holding the current back. It gives the highest figure the transformer could deliver, which is the safe side for choosing interrupting ratings. Every question here uses that method.

Question 1

A 750 kVA, 480 V three-phase transformer has an impedance of 5.75 percent. Assuming an unlimited supply on the primary, what fault current is available at its secondary terminals?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 15,689 A

Full-load current = 750,000 / (1.732 × 480) = 902 A. Fault current = full-load current / (%Z / 100) = 902 / 0.0575 = 15,689 A, about 15.7 kA.

Question 5

A transformer has an impedance of 5 percent. Assuming an unlimited primary supply, its secondary fault current is how many times its full-load current?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 20 times

The multiplier is 100 divided by the impedance percentage: 100 / 5 = 20 times. At 4 percent it is 25 times, and at 5.75 percent about 17.4 times.

Question 6

A 50 kVA, 240 V single-phase transformer has an impedance of 2.5 percent. Assuming an unlimited primary supply, what fault current is available at its secondary?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 8,333 A

Full-load current = 50,000 / 240 = 208 A. Fault current = 208 / 0.025 = 8,333 A. A small transformer with low impedance can still deliver several thousand amperes.

Question 7

A 1,000 kVA transformer has an impedance of 5.75 percent. What is its short-circuit capacity in MVA, assuming an unlimited primary supply?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 17.4 MVA

Short-circuit MVA = transformer MVA / (%Z / 100) = 1.0 / 0.0575 = 17.4 MVA. Working in MVA avoids the voltage and the 1.732 until the last step.

Question 8

A panelboard with a short-circuit current rating of 22 kA is to be connected directly to the secondary of a 1,500 kVA, 480 V transformer with 5.75 percent impedance. Is the rating adequate?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. No. About 31.4 kA is available, which is more than 22 kA.

Full-load current = 1,500,000 / (1.732 × 480) = 1,804 A. Available fault current = 1,804 / 0.0575 = 31,378 A, about 31.4 kA. That exceeds 22 kA, so the panelboard is not adequate at that point without a higher rating or a current-limiting device ahead of it. Conductor length between the two reduces the figure, and that has to be calculated, not assumed.
