Voltage Drop Calculations: Practice Questions

Voltage drop in volts and percent, single-phase, three-phase and DC, copper and aluminum, and sizing a conductor to a limit.

These all use the K-factor method: K = 12.9 for copper and 21.2 for aluminum, with the conductor area in circular mils given in each question. The multiplier is 2 for single-phase and DC, because the current goes out and back, and 1.732 for three-phase.

Question 1

A single-phase 240 V circuit runs 120 ft, one way, in 10 AWG copper (10,380 circular mils) and carries 30 A. What is the voltage drop?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 8.95 V

VD = 2 × K × I × L / cmil = 2 × 12.9 × 30 × 120 / 10,380 = 8.95 V, which is 3.73 percent of 240 V.

Question 2

A three-phase 208 V feeder runs 200 ft in 4 AWG copper (41,740 circular mils) and carries 60 A. What is the voltage drop as a percentage?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 3.09 %

VD = 1.732 × 12.9 × 60 × 200 / 41,740 = 6.42 V. As a percentage of 208 V that is 3.09 percent, above the 3 percent usually recommended for a feeder.

Question 3

A single-phase 240 V feeder runs 150 ft in 1/0 AWG aluminum (105,600 circular mils) and carries 100 A. What is the voltage drop?

Choices for question 3
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 6.02 V

Aluminum takes K = 21.2. VD = 2 × 21.2 × 100 × 150 / 105,600 = 6.02 V, or 2.51 percent.

Question 4

A 120 V two-wire circuit carries 15 A over a one-way distance of 150 ft. What is the smallest copper conductor area that keeps the drop to 3 percent?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 16,125 circular mils

Allowed drop = 0.03 × 120 = 3.6 V. cmil = 2 × K × I × L / VD = 2 × 12.9 × 15 × 150 / 3.6 = 16,125 circular mils. A 10 AWG conductor has 10,380, which is too small, so 8 AWG at 16,510 is the size to use.

Question 5

For a 3 percent drop on a 480 V three-phase feeder carrying 90 A in 2 AWG copper (66,360 circular mils), what is the longest run?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 475 ft

Allowed drop = 0.03 × 480 = 14.4 V. L = VD × cmil / (1.732 × K × I) = 14.4 × 66,360 / (1.732 × 12.9 × 90) = 475 ft, rounded down.

Question 6

A 48 V DC circuit runs 25 ft, one way, in 6 AWG copper (26,240 circular mils) and carries 40 A. What is the voltage drop as a percentage?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 2.05 %

VD = 2 × 12.9 × 40 × 25 / 26,240 = 0.98 V. As a percentage of 48 V that is 2.05 percent. The same drop in volts would be a much smaller percentage on a higher-voltage system.

Question 7

A circuit has too much voltage drop. The conductor is replaced with one of twice the cross-sectional area. What happens to the voltage drop?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. It halves.

Voltage drop is inversely proportional to conductor area, so doubling the area halves the drop. In AWG terms, twice the area is three sizes larger.

Question 8

A load is moved from 120 V to 240 V and draws the same power. With the same conductors and distance, what happens to the percentage voltage drop?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. It falls to one quarter.

At twice the voltage the same power takes half the current, so the drop in volts halves. That smaller drop is then measured against twice the voltage, so as a percentage it is one quarter. This is the main reason long runs are fed at the highest practical voltage.
