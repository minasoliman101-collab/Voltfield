Solar and Storage: Practice Questions

Array capacity, DC/AC ratio, clipping, amp-hours and kWh, C-rate and runtime.

Solar and storage sizing is mostly ratios: array to inverter, power to energy, usable to nameplate. Each question gives every figure it needs.

Question 1

An array has 240 modules rated 540 W each. What is its DC capacity?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 129.6 kWp

240 × 540 W = 129,600 W = 129.6 kWp.

Question 2

That 129.6 kWp array is connected to a 100 kW inverter. What is the DC/AC ratio?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 1.30

DC/AC ratio = array DC capacity / inverter AC rating = 129.6 / 100 = 1.30. Dividing the other way gives 0.77, the inverse.

Question 3

What is inverter clipping?

Choices for question 3
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. The inverter limiting its output to its AC rating when the array could supply more.

When the array offers more than the inverter can convert, the inverter moves off the maximum power point and the output flattens at its rating. The energy is never produced; nothing is damaged. It is usually a deliberate consequence of a DC/AC ratio above 1.0.

Question 4

A battery is rated 100 Ah at a nominal 48 V. What is its nameplate energy?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 4.80 kWh

kWh = Ah × V / 1,000 = 100 × 48 / 1,000 = 4.80 kWh. Amp-hours cannot be converted to energy without the voltage.

Question 5

A storage project is rated 100 MW and 400 MWh. What is its C-rate?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. 0.25C

C-rate is power divided by energy: 100 / 400 = 0.25C. The inverse, four, is the duration in hours, which is how such a project is usually described.

Question 6

A battery system is discharged at a steady 0.5C. About how long does a full discharge take?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 2 hours

Duration in hours is one divided by the C-rate: 1 / 0.5 = 2 hours. At 1C it is one hour, and at 0.25C four.

Question 7

A 100 Ah, 48 V battery may be discharged to 80 percent depth and feeds a 1,000 W load through an inverter that is 90 percent efficient. Estimate the runtime.

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 3.46 hours

Usable energy = 100 × 48 × 0.80 × 0.90 = 3,456 Wh. Runtime = 3,456 / 1,000 = 3.46 hours. Leaving out the inverter loss gives 3.84, and leaving out the depth of discharge gives 4.32.

Related: Battery Runtime Calculator

Question 8

A system must deliver 2,000 kWh of usable energy. With a 90 percent depth of discharge and 90 percent round-trip efficiency, what nameplate energy is needed?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 2,469 kWh

Nameplate = usable / (DoD × efficiency) = 2,000 / (0.90 × 0.90) = 2,469 kWh. Multiplying by the two factors instead of dividing gives a nameplate smaller than the usable energy, which cannot be right.
