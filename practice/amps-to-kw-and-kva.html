Amps to kW and kVA: Practice Questions

Working from a measured current to real power, apparent power and power factor, single-phase and three-phase.

A clamp meter gives amperes. Turning that into kilowatts or kVA takes the voltage, the number of phases and, for kilowatts only, the power factor. These questions run the conversion in that direction, and the last two work back to the power factor itself.

Question 1

A feeder carries 200 A per phase at 480 V, three-phase, with a power factor of 0.85. What real power is it delivering?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 141.3 kW

kW = 1.732 × V × I × PF / 1,000 = 1.732 × 480 × 200 × 0.85 / 1,000 = 141.3 kW.

Question 2

A panel feeder carries 150 A per phase at 208 V, three-phase. What is the apparent power?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 54.0 kVA

kVA = 1.732 × V × I / 1,000 = 1.732 × 208 × 150 / 1,000 = 54.0 kVA. No power factor is needed for apparent power.

Question 6

A single-phase 240 V circuit carries 40 A to a resistance heater. What power is the heater drawing?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 9.60 kW

For a resistive load, kW = V × I / 1,000 = 240 × 40 / 1,000 = 9.60 kW.

Question 7

A three-phase load draws 120 A at 480 V, and a power meter shows 85 kW. What is its power factor?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 0.85

kVA = 1.732 × 480 × 120 / 1,000 = 99.8 kVA. Power factor = kW / kVA = 85 / 99.8 = 0.85.
