Three-Phase Power and Power Factor: Practice Questions

Line current, kVA, kW, kvar, power factor correction and the square root of three.

Most wrong answers in three-phase work come from one of two places: leaving out the square root of three, or mixing up kW and kVA. Each question below has a wrong choice built from exactly those mistakes.

Question 1

A balanced three-phase load draws 75 kW at 480 V with a power factor of 0.9. What is the line current?

Choices for question 1
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 100.2 A

I = kW × 1,000 / (1.732 × V × PF) = 75,000 / (1.732 × 480 × 0.9) = 100.2 A. Leaving out the 1.732 gives 173.6 A, and leaving out the power factor gives 90.2 A.

Related: kW to Amps Calculator

Question 2

A three-phase feeder carries 100 A per phase at 480 V. What is the apparent power?

Choices for question 2
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 83.1 kVA

kVA = 1.732 × V × I / 1,000 = 1.732 × 480 × 100 / 1,000 = 83.1 kVA. No power factor is needed: apparent power is volts times amps.

Related: Amps to kVA Calculator

Question 3

A load draws 400 kW at a power factor of 0.8. What is its apparent power?

Choices for question 3
Show the worked solution

Answer: D. 500 kVA

kVA = kW / PF = 400 / 0.8 = 500 kVA. Multiplying by the power factor goes the wrong way and gives a kVA smaller than the kW, which is never possible.

Related: kVA to kW Converter

Question 4

The same 400 kW load at a power factor of 0.8 draws how much reactive power?

Choices for question 4
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 300 kvar

kvar = kW × tan(arccos PF). At 0.8 the tangent is 0.75, so kvar = 400 × 0.75 = 300 kvar. It also follows from the power triangle: √(500² − 400²) = 300.

Related: kVA to kW Converter

Question 5

A 480 V three-phase wye system has what voltage from any line to neutral?

Choices for question 5
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 277 V

Line-to-neutral voltage is line-to-line voltage divided by 1.732: 480 / 1.732 = 277 V. That is why the system is written 480Y/277.

Related: Delta vs. Wye

Question 6

A 400 kW load runs at a power factor of 0.75. How much capacitance is needed to raise it to 0.95?

Choices for question 6
Show the worked solution

Answer: A. 221.3 kvar

kvar = kW × (tan θ₁ − tan θ₂) = 400 × (0.8819 − 0.3287) = 221.3 kvar. Multiplying the kW by the difference in power factors, 0.20, is not a valid shortcut.

Related: Power Factor Correction Calculator

Question 7

In a delta-connected winding, how does line current compare with the current in each winding?

Choices for question 7
Show the worked solution

Answer: B. Line current is 1.732 times the winding current.

In a delta, each line is fed by two windings, and the two currents are 120 degrees apart, so they add to 1.732 times either one. Voltage is the quantity that is equal in a delta: line voltage is winding voltage. In a wye it is the other way round.

Related: Delta vs. Wye

Question 8

What is the full-load current of a 50 kVA three-phase supply at 208 V?

Choices for question 8
Show the worked solution

Answer: C. 138.8 A

I = kVA × 1,000 / (1.732 × V) = 50,000 / (1.732 × 208) = 138.8 A.
