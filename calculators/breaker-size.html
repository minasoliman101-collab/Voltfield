Breaker Size Calculator

Size a circuit breaker from the load: 125% of the continuous load plus the non-continuous load, rounded up to the next standard rating.

Breaker Size

125% of the continuous load plus the non-continuous load, rounded up to the next standard rating in NEC 240.6(A). Choose 100% rated only where the breaker and enclosure are listed for it. Not for motor circuits.

A standard breaker is sized at 125 percent of the continuous load plus 100 percent of the non-continuous load, then rounded up to the next standard rating. A continuous load is one expected to run at its maximum for three hours or more. The 125 percent figure is the same rule as "load a breaker to only 80 percent", seen from the other side.

The exception is a breaker and enclosure listed together for operation at 100 percent of rating. With one of those, the continuous load is taken at 100 percent. Such assemblies are common in large frame sizes and uncommon in small ones, and the listing has to say so; it is not a property of the breaker alone.

The formula

Minimum rating = (continuous load × 1.25) + non-continuous load
Breaker = next standard ampere rating at or above the minimum
Continuous loadCurrent expected to continue at its maximum for three hours or more, in amperes
Non-continuous loadEverything else on the circuit, in amperes
Standard ratingThe standard ampere ratings listed in NEC 240.6(A): 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 110, 125, 150, 175, 200 and upward

Worked example

A feeder carries 52 A of continuous load and nothing else:

52 × 1.25 = 65 A minimum. There is no 65 A standard rating, so the next one up is 70 A.

Add 10 A of non-continuous load to the same feeder:

(52 × 1.25) + 10 = 75 A minimum, and the next standard rating is 80 A.

With a 100 percent rated assembly the first case would be 52 A, and the next standard rating is 60 A.

Which standard governs this

NEC 210.20(A) for branch circuits and 215.3 for feeders set the 125 percent requirement and the exception for assemblies listed at 100 percent. NEC 240.6(A) lists the standard ampere ratings. NEC 240.4(B) permits the next higher standard rating where the conductor ampacity falls between two ratings, for devices rated 800 A or less; above 800 A, 240.4(C) requires the conductor ampacity to be at least the device rating.

What this calculation does not account for

This sizes the breaker from the load. It does not size the conductor, and the breaker also has to protect the conductor at its ampacity. It does not cover motor circuits, where NEC Article 430 sets branch-circuit protection as a percentage of full-load current to let the motor start, or transformer protection under Article 450. It says nothing about interrupting rating, which depends on the available fault current, not the load.

This is a screening estimate. It is here to get you to the right order of magnitude and the right conversation — not to replace a stamped calculation by a qualified engineer.

Common mistakes

Sizing the breaker to the load with no 125 percent factor on the continuous part. Applying the 125 percent factor twice, once to the load and again to the breaker. Assuming a breaker is 100 percent rated because of its frame size. Choosing the breaker and then forgetting to check that the conductor it protects has the ampacity. Using this method on a motor circuit.
