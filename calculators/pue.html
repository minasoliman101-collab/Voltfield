PUE Calculator

Calculate data center PUE and DCiE from total facility power and IT power, with the overhead in kW and what it costs over a year.

PUE

Both figures in kW, measured over the same period. The energy rate is optional and prices the overhead for a year.

PUE is total facility power divided by IT equipment power. A PUE of 1.5 means that for every kilowatt delivered to servers, storage and network equipment, another half kilowatt goes to cooling, power conversion losses, lighting and everything else in the building. A PUE of 1.0 would mean no overhead at all, which no real facility reaches.

The number is only as good as the two measurements behind it. Both have to cover the same period, the total has to be taken at the utility meter or its equivalent, and the IT figure should be taken as close to the equipment as the metering allows.

The formula

PUE = total facility power / IT equipment power
DCiE = 1 / PUE, as a percentage
Overhead = total facility power − IT equipment power
Total facility powerEverything the data center draws, including cooling, UPS and distribution losses and lighting, in kW
IT equipment powerPower delivered to servers, storage and network equipment, in kW
DCiEData center infrastructure efficiency, the reciprocal of PUE

Worked example

A facility draws 1,500 kW in total and delivers 1,000 kW to IT equipment:

PUE = 1,500 / 1,000 = 1.50, and DCiE = 66.7%

Overhead = 1,500 − 1,000 = 500 kW

Held for a year, that overhead is 500 × 8,760 = 4,380,000 kWh. At $0.08 per kWh it costs about $350,400. Bringing the same IT load to a PUE of 1.3 would cut the overhead to 300 kW and save 1,752,000 kWh a year.

Which standard governs this

PUE was introduced by The Green Grid and is standardized as ISO/IEC 30134-2, which defines the measurement points and requires the figure to be based on energy over a full year. A PUE taken from instantaneous power, as this calculator does, is a snapshot and should be labelled as one.

What this calculation does not account for

A snapshot PUE moves with the weather and with IT load, so a single reading can be well away from the annual figure. PUE also says nothing about how efficiently the IT equipment uses the power it receives, and it can improve simply because IT load went up while overhead stayed the same. It does not account for water use or for the carbon intensity of the supply.

This is a screening estimate. It is here to get you to the right order of magnitude and the right conversation — not to replace a stamped calculation by a qualified engineer.

Common mistakes

Comparing a design PUE at full load with a measured PUE at partial load. Measuring IT power at the UPS output and so counting distribution losses as IT load. Quoting the best winter reading as the facility's PUE. Comparing PUE between sites in different climates as though it measured the quality of the design alone.
