Motor Torque Calculator

Calculate motor torque in lb-ft and N·m from horsepower or kW and speed in rpm, with the 5252 constant explained.

Motor Torque

T = 5,252 × hp ÷ rpm. Running torque at the power and speed entered.

Torque in pound-feet is 5,252 times horsepower divided by rpm. In SI units, torque in newton-metres is 9,549 times kilowatts divided by rpm. For a given power, a slower shaft carries more torque, which is why a six-pole motor is physically larger than a two-pole motor of the same rating.

The 5,252 is not a property of motors. It comes from the definition of horsepower, 33,000 foot-pounds per minute, divided by 2π to turn revolutions into radians.

The formula

T (lb-ft) = 5,252 × hp / rpm
T (N·m) = 9,549 × kW / rpm
TTorque at the shaft
hp, kWShaft output power
rpmShaft speed in revolutions per minute

The equipment

Worked example

A 50 hp motor running at its full-load speed of 1,775 rpm:

T = 5,252 × 50 / 1,775 = 147.9 lb-ft, which is 200.6 N·m

A 50 hp motor with six poles runs near 1,180 rpm and delivers 222.5 lb-ft: the same power, half again the torque.

Which standard governs this

The relation is definitional. The torque figures that matter for applying a motor, locked-rotor, pull-up and breakdown torque as percentages of full-load torque, are set for each design letter by NEMA MG 1, and by IEC 60034-12 for IEC motors.

What this calculation does not account for

This is full-load running torque at the speed you enter. It says nothing about the torque available during starting, which follows its own curve from locked-rotor torque at standstill, through a dip, up to breakdown torque near full speed. On a variable speed drive, a motor can generally deliver rated torque up to base speed and less above it, where it runs at constant power.

This is a screening estimate. It is here to get you to the right order of magnitude and the right conversation — not to replace a stamped calculation by a qualified engineer.

Common mistakes

Using synchronous speed in place of full-load speed. Confusing full-load torque with starting torque when checking whether a load will break away. Mixing horsepower with the SI constant or kilowatts with 5,252. Forgetting that a gearbox multiplies torque by its ratio and divides speed by the same figure.
