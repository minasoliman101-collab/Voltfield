Pump Power Calculator

Pump Power

Water hp = GPM × head × SG ÷ 3,960. One duty point; check the motor against the whole pump curve.Water power in kW = L/s × head in metres × SG ÷ 102. One duty point; check the motor against the whole pump curve.

Pump power is flow times head divided by 3,960, divided again by the pump's efficiency. Flow in GPM times head in feet over 3,960 gives the power delivered to the water. Dividing by pump efficiency gives the brake horsepower the shaft has to supply, and dividing that by motor efficiency gives the electrical input.

Head is the pressure the pump adds, expressed as the height of a column of the liquid. It is not the height the water is lifted, unless the system is open. In a closed chilled water loop the static heights cancel and the head is all friction: pipe, fittings, valves, the coil and the chiller.

Pump power in kilowatts is flow in litres per second times head in metres, divided by 102, and divided again by the pump's efficiency. Flow times head over 102 gives the power delivered to the water. Dividing by pump efficiency gives the power the shaft has to supply, and dividing that by motor efficiency gives the electrical input.

The formula

Water hp = GPM × head (ft) × SG / 3,960
Brake hp = water hp / pump efficiency
Input kW = brake hp × 0.7457 / motor efficiency

HeadTotal dynamic head in feet of liquid
SGSpecific gravity. 1.0 for water
Pump efficiencyFrom the pump curve at the duty point, as a fraction
Motor efficiencyFrom the motor nameplate, as a fraction

Shaft kW = water kW / pump efficiency
Input kW = shaft kW / motor efficiency

HTotal dynamic head in metres of liquid

The equipment

Worked example

A chilled water pump delivering 500 GPM against 80 ft of head, with a pump efficiency of 75 percent and a motor efficiency of 93 percent:

Water hp = 500 × 80 × 1.0 / 3,960 = 10.1 hp

Brake hp = 10.1 / 0.75 = 13.5 hp

Input kW = 13.5 × 0.7457 / 0.93 = 10.8 kW

The next standard motor at or above 13.5 hp is 15 hp. Running all year, 10.8 kW is about 94,600 kWh.

A chilled water pump delivering 30 L/s against 24 m of head, with a pump efficiency of 75 percent and a motor efficiency of 93 percent:

Water kW = 30 × 24 × 1.0 / 102 = 7.1 kW

Shaft kW = 7.1 / 0.75 = 9.4 kW

Input kW = 9.4 / 0.93 = 10.1 kW

The next standard IEC motor at or above 9.4 kW is 11 kW. Running all year, 10.1 kW is about 88,500 kWh.

Which standard governs this

The equations are the standard pump relations published by the Hydraulic Institute and in the ASHRAE Handbook. Motor efficiency classes and standard horsepower ratings come from NEMA MG 1. ASHRAE Standard 90.1 limits pumping power in building hydronic systems and requires variable flow above certain sizes.

What this calculation does not account for

This is one operating point. A pump runs wherever its curve crosses the system curve, and the motor has to be large enough for every point the pump may reach, including runout at low head. The efficiency you enter must be the one at the duty point; away from the best efficiency point it falls quickly. The calculation does not check net positive suction head, which decides whether the pump cavitates.

This is a screening estimate. It is here to get you to the right order of magnitude and the right conversation — not to replace a stamped calculation by a qualified engineer.

Common mistakes

Using the height of the building as the head on a closed loop. Using the pump's peak efficiency for a duty point far from it. Selecting a motor for the design point with nothing in hand for runout. Forgetting specific gravity on a glycol system. Adding the heads of two pumps in parallel, where it is the flows that add.
