Changelog

What's new

A dated log of what's shipped — new guides, calculators, and tools, in the order they landed. Most recent first. Recent themes: NEC and standards references written method-first, calculators that show their working, and giving each tool a page of its own.

November 11, 2026
  • The home page and the header now say what the site covers: electrical and cooling. The home page has a section on the cooling side, and the Tools menu links to the reference charts and practice questions from every page.
  • Practice questions: 64 multiple-choice questions in 8 sets, from Ohm's law and three-phase power to transformers and data center cooling. Every numerical answer is computed from the formula in its solution, and each wrong choice is what a specific mistake produces, which the solution names.
  • Metric units. Ten calculators that worked only in feet, inches, GPM, gallons or degrees Fahrenheit now have a US / Metric switch, among them voltage drop, pump power, pipe velocity, airflow and arc flash. The choice is remembered across tools, results lead with the system in use, and the pump calculator picks the next IEC motor size in kW. Shared links mean the same thing in either system.
  • Forty-five glossary terms now have a page of their own, with the definition, how the term is used in practice, the points people get wrong, and links to the calculators and guides that go with it. For example transformer impedance, interrupting rating, C-rate and N+1 and 2N.
  • Calculator results can be shared and carried forward. After a calculation the page address holds the inputs, so the link reopens the same result, and there are buttons for “Copy link to this result” and “Copy result”. Under the result, next-step links take the answer into the tool that follows: a current from kW to amps goes on to wire size, breaker size and voltage drop; a flow from chilled water flow goes on to pump power and pipe velocity.
  • Reference charts: eight printable pages, each computed from a formula shown on it and downloadable as CSV. AWG Wire Size, kVA to Amps, kW to Amps, breaker Size, voltage Drop, motor Speed, HP to kW, cooling Load. There is deliberately no ampacity or motor full-load current chart: those are code tables, and the page says so.
  • Home page 3D models now move: fans spin, the motor shaft turns and status lamps pulse, with labelled callouts on each model.
  • The mechanical side of the data center. Three guides: CRAC vs. CRAH, hot aisle vs. cold aisle containment and the pump and fan affinity laws, including where static head stops the cube law from applying. Five calculators to go with them: airflow for a heat load, chilled water flow, pump power, pipe velocity and affinity laws.
  • Three motor calculators: torque, synchronous speed and slip, and voltage unbalance by the NEMA method.
  • New 3D models of a centrifugal pump, an air-cooled chiller and a cooling tower. The pump, chiller, cooling-unit and motor models can be opened from the calculators that size them.
  • Ten new calculators, each on its own page with the method and a worked example: Ohm's law, breaker size, wire resistance, amps to kVA, amps to watts, Ah to kWh, battery runtime, PUE, electricity cost and solar DC/AC ratio. The battery runtime tool reports the discharge rate and says when its estimate stops being trustworthy.
  • Calculators now open on a worked example with the result shown, instead of an empty form.
  • New contact and terms of use pages, linked with the privacy policy from the foot of every page.
November 4, 2026
  • Four guides on questions where the published answers contradict each other. SPD Types 1, 2 and 3 — the type number is a permitted location, not a quality grade, and Type 1 is the only one allowed on the supply side of the service disconnect. NEMA Enclosure Types — the numbers are not a ladder, Type 12 is indoor while the lower-numbered Type 3R is outdoor, and an IP code cannot be converted into a Type rating. Panelboard vs. Switchboard vs. Switchgear — no ampere threshold separates them; the listing standard and the short-time rating do. NEC 220.87 — measuring an existing load instead of calculating it.
  • Six more guides: NEC 705 supply-side vs. load-side, including the breaker position the 120% allowance depends on and the 100% case that applies without it; interrupting rating vs. SCCR; NEC 240.87 arc energy reduction; EV load management under NEC 625.42; VFD vs. soft starter; and ATS vs. STS vs. static bypass.
  • Three converters that were buried inside other pages now have their own, with more capability than before. AWG to mm² is computed from the AWG geometric definition across the full range rather than from a 21-row table, and names the nearest IEC standard size and which way it rounds. Conduit fill now takes mixed conductor sizes, which is the normal case and the one the old widget could not do. kVA to kW solves the whole power triangle from any two values and checks a third against them.
  • Two new calculators: three-phase power, which solves for whichever value is unknown and separates line from phase figures for delta and wye, and BTU, tons and kW, including IT load to cooling tons.
  • Correction: the NEMA-to-IP cross-reference on Reference Tables presented a two-way equivalence. It now states that the mapping reads in one direction only — a NEMA Type can meet or exceed an IP degree, but an IP degree never equals a Type — and notes that NEC 110.28 does not accept IP ratings as a substitute.
November 3, 2026
  • Six new calculators, each on its own page with the method and a worked example below the tool: amps to kW, kW to amps, kVA to amps, HP to kW, watts to amps, and wire size. The wire size calculator runs both checks the NEC actually requires — the termination temperature limit at 125% of a continuous load, and ambient plus bundling adjustment against the real load — and reports whichever demands more copper.
  • Three comparison guides: delta vs. wye, ground vs. neutral, and fuse vs. circuit breaker.
  • Three NEC guides written method-first, without reproducing the code tables: 110.26 working space, 250.122 equipment grounding conductor sizing, and 430.250 motor full-load current.
  • Three data-center reference guides: Uptime Institute Tiers, which leads on the fact that “Tier” is a trademark and the familiar availability percentages are not in the current standard; ANSI device numbers; and rack power density.
