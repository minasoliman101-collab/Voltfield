Engineering Calculators for Power & Energy Procurement

Seven client-side calculators for early-stage sizing and budgetary planning — cable sizing, transformer & BESS sizing, solar strings, fault current, power factor correction, and unit conversion. Standard formulas, your inputs, nothing sent to a server.

These tools are built for the budgetary and early-design stage of a project — sizing a feeder, roughing in a transformer or BESS order, or checking a string configuration before it goes to a stamped design. They run entirely in your browser: nothing you enter is sent anywhere. None of them replace a licensed engineer's calculation for final design, protective device coordination, or code compliance sign-off.

Voltage Drop / Cable Sizing Calculator

Estimates voltage drop across a feeder using the standard K-factor method, then flags runs that exceed the commonly used 3% branch-circuit / 5% total design guideline (NEC 210.19(A) informational note — a recommendation, not a hard limit).

VD = (2 × K × I × D) / CM  [single-phase]  ·  VD = (1.732 × K × I × D) / CM  [three-phase]

Voltage Drop

K = 12.9 (copper) or 21.2 (aluminum) ohm·cmil/ft — approximate, ignores conductor reactance. Best for runs under ~250 ft.

Transformer Sizing Calculator

Converts a real load in kW to required kVA and rounds up to the nearest standard ANSI/IEEE C57.12.00 transformer nameplate rating, plus estimated full-load amps on the secondary.

kVA = kW / PF  ·  FLA = kVA × 1000 / (1.732 × V)  [3φ]  or  kVA × 1000 / V  [1φ]

Transformer Size

Rounds up to the nearest standard kVA nameplate rating. Add your own margin for future load growth — this doesn't assume any.

BESS Sizing Calculator

Converts a critical load and backup duration into usable energy, then to the nameplate energy rating you'd need to procure once depth of discharge and round-trip efficiency losses are factored in. See Battery Storage for DC block configurations.

Usable kWh = kW × hours  ·  Nameplate kWh = Usable kWh / (DoD × RTE)

BESS Size

Defaults (90% DoD, 90% RTE) are typical for utility-scale LFP systems — adjust to your spec sheet.

Solar String Sizing Calculator

Applies module temperature coefficients to your site's design temperatures to find the maximum string length (cold-weather Voc must stay under the inverter's max input voltage) and minimum string length (hot-weather Vmp must stay in the inverter's MPPT window).

Voc(cold) = Voc × (1 + (Tc·Voc/100) × (Tmin−25))  ·  Vmp(hot) = Vmp × (1 + (Tc·Vmp/100) × (Tmax−25))

String Size

All values from the module and inverter datasheets — this tool does no lookups of its own.

Fault Current Estimate

A simplified, infinite-source estimate of available symmetrical fault current at a transformer's secondary terminals — useful for a first-pass equipment rating check, not a substitute for a full short-circuit/coordination study that accounts for source and cable impedance.

Isc = (100,000 × kVA) / (1.732 × V × %Z)

Available Fault Current

%Z comes from the transformer nameplate — typical distribution transformers run 5.75%–6.5%, but always use the actual nameplate value.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate voltage drop for a cable run?

Using the K-factor method: VD = (2 × K × I × D) / CM for single-phase or VD = (1.732 × K × I × D) / CM for three-phase, where K is a resistivity constant (about 12.9 for copper, 21.2 for aluminum), I is load current in amps, D is one-way distance in feet, and CM is the conductor's circular mil area. This is an approximation that ignores reactance and is best suited to feeders under about 250 feet.

What size transformer do I need for my load?

Divide your real load in kW by its power factor to get required kVA, then round up to the nearest standard transformer nameplate rating (common ANSI/IEEE sizes include 75, 112.5, 150, 225, 300, 500, 750, and 1000 kVA). Always leave margin for future load growth.

How do I size a battery energy storage system (BESS)?

Multiply the critical load in kW by the required backup duration in hours to get usable energy in kWh, then divide by your assumed depth of discharge and round-trip efficiency (commonly around 90% each for LFP systems) to get the nameplate energy rating you need to procure.

Are these calculators a substitute for a stamped engineering study?

No. These tools use standard simplified formulas for early-stage budgetary sizing and procurement planning. Final designs, especially fault current and arc-flash coordination, require a licensed engineer's study using actual system impedances.
