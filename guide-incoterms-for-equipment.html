Incoterms 2020 for Heavy Equipment: EXW, FCA, DAP and DDP Explained

Three letters on a purchase order decide who books the truck, who insures the load and who owns the problem when a transformer arrives with a cracked bushing. This is what each rule means and which ones fit engineered equipment.

By Voltfield Editorial Team·Published November 15, 2026·How we source & verify this

Key takeaways

  • An Incoterms rule sets three things: who arranges and pays for carriage, where risk of loss passes from seller to buyer, and who handles export and import clearance.
  • It does not set when title passes, when payment is due or what happens if the goods are defective. Those belong in the purchase order.
  • The rule is incomplete without a named place. “DAP” means nothing; “DAP Taylor, TX site, on truck” does.
  • FOB, FAS, CFR and CIF are written for sea freight. For trucks and containers use FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU or DDP.

What an Incoterms rule decides

Incoterms are eleven standard trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce. Each one is a three-letter rule that divides the cost and the risk of moving goods between a seller and a buyer. The current edition is Incoterms 2020. A rule answers three questions: who arranges and pays for transport, at what point the risk of loss or damage moves from seller to buyer, and who is responsible for export and import formalities.

The point where risk passes is the one that matters when something goes wrong. If a transformer is dropped after risk has passed to you, you still owe the seller the price and your remedy is against the carrier or your insurer. If it is dropped before, it is the seller’s loss to sort out.

Just as important is what the rules leave out. They say nothing about when ownership passes, how or when the price is paid, warranty, or late delivery. A purchase order that relies on an Incoterms rule for any of those has a gap in it.

The rules, from least to most seller responsibility

Seven rules work for any mode of transport and are the ones to use for equipment moving by truck, rail, container or a mix:

Four more, FAS, FOB, CFR and CIF, are for sea and inland waterway transport only. Their delivery points are alongside or on board a vessel, which is the wrong place to draw the line for a container that was handed over at an inland terminal days earlier.

CPT and CIP catch people out. The seller pays the freight all the way to the destination, but the risk passed to the buyer back when the goods were handed to the first carrier. Paying for the journey is not the same as carrying its risk.

Which rule fits heavy equipment

For a transformer, a generator or a lineup of switchgear, the practical question is who is better placed to manage a difficult move: permits, escorts, a specialized carrier, and proof of what happened on the way.

DAP to the site is the common choice. The manufacturer arranges a carrier that moves its product every week, the risk in transit stays with the party that packed and blocked the load, and the buyer takes over at the gate. The buyer supplies the crane and the rigging crew, and the risk of the lift is the buyer’s.

DPU moves the unloading to the seller as well. It suits a buyer with no rigging capability on site, and it needs the place defined exactly: on the pad, on the laydown area, or just off the truck.

FCA at the factory suits a buyer with its own heavy-haul contract and logistics team. Risk passes at the factory dock, so the buyer needs cargo insurance in place before the load moves, and a clear record of the condition at handover.

EXW looks cheapest and is usually the wrong choice. The seller is not obliged to load the goods, and for an export sale the buyer is left to clear export from a country where it may have no standing to do so. FCA fixes both problems for very little.

DDP on an imported unit hands the duties and import clearance to the seller. That is convenient, but check that the seller is actually able to act as importer in your country, and remember that whoever is importer of record carries the compliance obligations that go with it. Projects with domestic content requirements should read the Buy America and FEOC guides alongside this one.

The named place

Every rule is followed by a place, and the precision of that place is what makes the rule work. “DAP Texas” leaves room for an argument about whether the seller’s job ended at a rail terminal or at your gate. Write the place the way you would give directions to the driver: the site name and address, and where on the site.

Add what the rule itself does not say. State who unloads, even where the rule implies it. State the delivery window and how much notice the site needs. State who supplies the impact recorder and who reads it on arrival. And state the edition: “Incoterms 2020”.

A note on “FOB” in the United States

Inside the United States, “FOB origin” and “FOB destination” are long-standing commercial terms that come from the Uniform Commercial Code, not from Incoterms. They are used for truck freight every day, and they mean something different from the Incoterms FOB rule, which is about loading a ship. A domestic order that says “FOB destination, freight prepaid” is clear to both sides. An order that mixes the two systems, for example “FOB factory, Incoterms 2020” for a truck shipment, is not. Pick one system and name it.

Common mistakes

  • A rule with no named place, or a place as vague as a city.
  • Assuming the rule settles title or payment. It settles neither.
  • Assuming CPT or CIP means the seller carries the transit risk. The seller pays the freight; the buyer carries the risk from the first carrier onward.
  • Using FOB or CIF for containers or trucks.
  • Buying EXW and discovering nobody is contracted to load the truck.
  • Freight terms on the bill of lading that contradict the order. Prepaid or collect on the bill of lading should follow from the rule on the purchase order.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between DAP and DDP?

Under both, the seller delivers the goods to the named place ready for unloading and carries the risk until then. Under DDP the seller also clears the goods for import and pays the duties and taxes. Under DAP import clearance and duties are the buyer’s.

What is the difference between EXW and FCA?

Under EXW the seller only makes the goods available at its premises: it does not have to load them or clear them for export. Under FCA the seller loads the goods onto the buyer’s carrier when delivery is at the seller’s premises, and clears them for export. FCA is usually the better choice for the same price.

Do Incoterms determine when ownership transfers?

No. Incoterms set the transfer of risk and the split of costs and tasks. When title passes is a matter for the contract and the governing law, so state it in the purchase order.

Which Incoterm should I use for a transformer?

There is no single answer, but DAP to the project site is a common choice because the manufacturer arranges the specialized transport and carries the transit risk, while the buyer unloads with its own crane. DPU adds unloading to the seller’s scope, and FCA suits a buyer that runs its own heavy-haul logistics.

Next steps

The rule and its named place go on the purchase order, the freight terms that follow from it go on the bill of lading, and the handover it describes is recorded on the proof of delivery.
