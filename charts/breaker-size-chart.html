Breaker Size Chart

Every standard breaker rating from 15 A to 1,200 A, the continuous load it may carry, and what that load is in kilowatts.

A standard breaker may carry a continuous load of 80 percent of its rating. That is the 125 percent sizing rule read backwards: a 20 A breaker takes 16 A of continuous load, a 100 A breaker takes 80 A. A continuous load is one expected to run at its maximum for three hours or more.

How to read it

To size a breaker for a load, find the first row whose continuous-load figure is at or above your load. A 52 A continuous load is more than a 60 A breaker allows (48 A), so it needs 70 A.

The kW columns answer the opposite question: how much a circuit can supply. A 20 A, 120 V circuit carries 1.92 kW continuously, which is why a 1,500 W heater is close to the limit of one.

Where a load is partly continuous and partly not, add 125 percent of the continuous part to the rest and choose the next standard rating at or above the total. The breaker size calculator does that sum.

How the values are calculated

Continuous load = breaker rating × 0.80
Minimum breaker = (continuous load × 1.25) + non-continuous load

Every figure in the chart comes from the formulas above. Nothing is copied from a published table, so any cell can be checked by hand.

What this chart does not tell you

The chart sizes a breaker from a load. It does not size the conductor, and the breaker must also protect the conductor at its ampacity. It does not apply to motor circuits, where NEC Article 430 sets protection by its own percentages, or to breakers in assemblies listed for operation at 100 percent of rating. It says nothing about interrupting rating, which is set by the available fault current.

This is a reference, not a design. It is here to give you the right order of magnitude and a figure to check against. It does not replace the adopted code or a calculation by a qualified engineer.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps can a 20 amp breaker handle?

A 20 A breaker can carry 20 A of non-continuous load, or 16 A of continuous load, meaning a load that runs at its maximum for three hours or more. On a 120 V circuit, 16 A is 1.92 kW.

What size breaker do I need for 50 amps?

If the 50 A load is continuous, multiply by 1.25 to get 62.5 A and take the next standard size, 70 A. If it is non-continuous, a 50 A breaker is enough. The conductor has to be sized to match.

Why can a breaker only be loaded to 80 percent?

A standard breaker is tested in open air and is used in an enclosure, where it runs hotter. The 125 percent rule keeps a continuous load within what the breaker can carry in an enclosure without nuisance tripping. Assemblies specifically listed for 100 percent operation are exempt.
