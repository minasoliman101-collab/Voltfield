Cooling Load Chart

A heat load in kilowatts expressed as BTU per hour and tons, and the airflow and chilled water flow needed to carry it away.

One kilowatt of heat is 3,412 BTU per hour, and one ton of cooling is 12,000 BTU per hour, or 3.517 kW. Nearly all the electricity an IT load draws leaves as heat, so a 100 kW room needs about 28.4 tons of sensible cooling before any allowance for other loads or redundancy.

How to read it

Start with the IT load in kW and read off the tons. Then use the second table for the air that load needs at your design temperature rise, and the third for the chilled water serving the cooling units.

The airflow and water flow tables show the same thing from two directions: the wider the temperature difference a system is designed for, the less fluid it has to move. Since fan and pump power fall with the cube of flow, this is where much of a cooling system's efficiency is decided.

How the values are calculated

BTU/h = kW × 3,412
Tons = BTU/h / 12,000
CFM = BTU/h / (1.08 × ΔT)
GPM = tons × 24 / ΔT

Every figure in the chart comes from the formulas above. Nothing is copied from a published table, so any cell can be checked by hand.

What this chart does not tell you

These are design-point figures for sensible heat. They leave out lighting, people, heat gain through the building, humidification and the cooling plant's own losses, and they include no redundancy. The air constant of 1.08 applies at sea level; at altitude more air is needed. The water constant applies to plain water, and a glycol mix needs more flow.

This is a reference, not a design. It is here to give you the right order of magnitude and a figure to check against. It does not replace the adopted code or a calculation by a qualified engineer.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons of cooling per kW?

One kW of heat needs 0.284 tons of cooling, because a ton is 3.517 kW. Put the other way, each ton of cooling removes 3.517 kW of heat. A 100 kW IT load needs about 28.4 tons.

How many CFM per kW of IT load?

It depends on the temperature rise across the equipment. At a 20°F rise it is about 158 CFM per kW; at 30°F it is about 105. Modern servers are often designed for the wider rise.

How many GPM per ton of chilled water?

24 divided by the temperature difference in °F. At the traditional 10°F difference that is 2.4 GPM per ton; at 12°F it is 2.0 and at 16°F it is 1.5.
