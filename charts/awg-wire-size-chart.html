AWG Wire Size Chart

Every standard size from 18 AWG to 1000 kcmil, with its diameter, its area in circular mils and square millimetres, and its resistance in copper and aluminum.

In the American Wire Gauge system a smaller number is a larger wire, and every three sizes the area doubles. A 10 AWG conductor has about twice the copper of a 13 AWG and half the resistance; go up six sizes and the diameter doubles. Above 4/0 the gauge numbers stop and sizes are given directly by area, in thousands of circular mils (kcmil).

Diameter is that of a solid round conductor of the stated area. A stranded conductor of the same size is slightly larger overall. Resistance is DC, at about 75°C.

How to read it

Area in circular mils is the diameter in thousandths of an inch, squared. A wire one mil across is one circular mil; a wire 100 mils across is 10,000. It is a unit invented to avoid π in the arithmetic.

Square millimetres are circular mils times 0.0005067. AWG sizes do not land on the standard metric sizes: 12 AWG is 3.31 mm², between the IEC sizes of 2.5 and 4 mm². When a metric cable replaces an AWG one, the next size up is the safe choice.

Resistance is K × 1000 / area, with K = 12.9 for copper and 21.2 for aluminum in ohm-circular mils per foot. That is the same approximation the voltage drop calculator uses, and it tracks the 75°C figures in NEC Chapter 9, Table 8 closely.

How the values are calculated

Area (cmil) = (diameter in mils)²
Area (mm²) = cmil × 0.0005067
R (Ω/1000 ft) = K × 1000 / cmil

Every figure in the chart comes from the formulas above. Nothing is copied from a published table, so any cell can be checked by hand.

What this chart does not tell you

This chart gives geometry and resistance. It does not give ampacity, which depends on insulation temperature rating, how many conductors share a raceway, the ambient temperature and the terminations, and has to come from the adopted code. Resistance is DC; on large conductors carrying AC, skin effect raises the effective figure.

This is a reference, not a design. It is here to give you the right order of magnitude and a figure to check against. It does not replace the adopted code or a calculation by a qualified engineer.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a smaller AWG number mean a bigger wire?

The gauge number originally counted the drawing steps used to make the wire. Each pass through a die made it thinner, so more passes, a higher number, meant a finer wire. The modern system keeps that direction and fixes the sizes by formula.

What comes after 4/0 AWG?

Sizes larger than 4/0 are given by area in thousands of circular mils: 250 kcmil, 300 kcmil and upward. 4/0 is 211,600 circular mils, so 250 kcmil is the next standard size. The older abbreviation MCM means the same as kcmil.

How do I convert AWG to mm²?

Multiply the area in circular mils by 0.0005067. 12 AWG is 6,530 cmil, which is 3.31 mm². Because AWG and metric sizes do not line up, a metric replacement is normally the next standard size above that figure.
