kVA to Amps Chart

Full-load current for the standard transformer sizes at the common voltages, three-phase and single-phase.

Three-phase amps are kVA times 1,000, divided by 1.732 times the line voltage. A 75 kVA transformer delivers 208 A at 208 V and 90 A at 480 V. There is no power factor in the conversion: kVA is already volts times amps.

How to read it

Find the transformer rating in the first column and read across to the voltage of the winding you are interested in. Each winding has its own current: a 500 kVA unit stepping 480 V down to 208 V carries 601 A on the primary and 1,388 A on the secondary.

These are full-load currents at rated kVA. A transformer serving a 400 kVA load carries 80 percent of the figure shown for 500 kVA.

How the values are calculated

I = kVA × 1000 / (1.732 × V)  [three-phase]
I = kVA × 1000 / V  [single-phase]

Every figure in the chart comes from the formulas above. Nothing is copied from a published table, so any cell can be checked by hand.

What this chart does not tell you

Full-load current is the starting point for sizing conductors and overcurrent protection, not the answer. NEC 450.3 sets transformer protection as a percentage of this current, conductors are sized under Article 310 with the 125 percent continuous-load factor where it applies, and the figure says nothing about inrush or the fault current the transformer can deliver.

This is a reference, not a design. It is here to give you the right order of magnitude and a figure to check against. It does not replace the adopted code or a calculation by a qualified engineer.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps is a 75 kVA transformer?

At full load, a three-phase 75 kVA transformer carries 208 A at 208 V, 180 A at 240 V and 90 A at 480 V. A single-phase 75 kVA unit carries 313 A at 240 V.

Do I need power factor to convert kVA to amps?

No. kVA is apparent power, which is voltage times current, so current follows directly. Power factor only enters when converting between kVA and kW.

Is the primary current the same as the secondary current?

No. The kVA is the same on both sides, but the current is inversely proportional to voltage. Look up each winding at its own voltage.
