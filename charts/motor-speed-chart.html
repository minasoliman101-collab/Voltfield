Motor Speed Chart

Synchronous speed for each pole count at 60 Hz and 50 Hz, and how much torque a horsepower is worth at that speed.

An AC motor's synchronous speed is 120 times the frequency divided by the number of poles. At 60 Hz that is 3,600 rpm for two poles and 1,800 for four. An induction motor runs a few percent below these figures at full load; the shortfall is its slip.

Torque per horsepower is at the 60 Hz synchronous speed; torque per kW is at the 50 Hz speed. Actual full-load torque is slightly higher, because the motor runs slightly slower.

How to read it

A nameplate speed of 1,750 or 1,775 rpm means a four-pole motor on 60 Hz, since the nearest synchronous speed above it is 1,800. The difference, 25 to 50 rpm, is the slip at full load.

Torque rises as speed falls. A 10 hp motor delivers about 15 lb-ft at two poles and about 58 lb-ft at eight. That is why a slow motor of the same horsepower is physically larger.

On a variable speed drive, synchronous speed follows the output frequency: a four-pole motor at 30 Hz has a synchronous speed of 900 rpm.

How the values are calculated

N = 120 × f / poles
T (lb-ft) = 5,252 × hp / rpm
T (N·m) = 9,549 × kW / rpm

Every figure in the chart comes from the formulas above. Nothing is copied from a published table, so any cell can be checked by hand.

What this chart does not tell you

These are synchronous speeds. The speed an induction motor actually runs at depends on its load and its design, and comes from the nameplate. Torque figures are running torque at rated power; starting and breakdown torque are separate values set by the motor's design letter.

This is a reference, not a design. It is here to give you the right order of magnitude and a figure to check against. It does not replace the adopted code or a calculation by a qualified engineer.

Frequently asked questions

What rpm is a 4 pole motor?

Its synchronous speed is 1,800 rpm at 60 Hz and 1,500 rpm at 50 Hz. A four-pole induction motor at full load typically runs between about 1,725 and 1,780 rpm on 60 Hz.

How do I tell how many poles a motor has?

From the nameplate speed. Find the synchronous speed just above it in the chart: about 3,500 rpm is two poles, about 1,750 is four, about 1,150 is six and about 870 is eight, all at 60 Hz.

Why does a slower motor have more torque?

Power is torque times speed. For the same power, halving the speed doubles the torque. The motor needs a larger rotor and frame to produce that torque, which is why low-speed motors are bigger and more expensive.
